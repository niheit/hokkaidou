|  INTRODUCTION  |

 ---------------- 
I worked for Hokkaidou University from 2010 to 2021.  At the time, I 
guided many excursions.  I made this file based on my excursion notes.  
This is for virtual excursions that transcend time and space.  ( by T. 
Nihei ) 

In this file, place names are written as they are pronounced, for 
example, Hokkaido --> Hokkaidou.  This is because the content is for 
oral explanation ( by Nihei lab 2025 ). 

Some place names are displayed in UTF codes.  To convert UTF codes to 
characters, use the ``echo'' command on a terminal emulator, for 
example ; echo $'\u5317\u7ADC' 

Or, these online command-line interpreters are available ;
http://codapi.org/shell/
http://www.tutorialspoint.com/compilers/online-bash-compiler.htm

|  MAP 1 - NO. 1  |
 -----------------
LOCATION

Oshima ( o-shi-ma ) ( \u6E21\u5CF6 ) is a sub-prefecture ( ``sougou-
shinkou-kyoku'' ) in south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  Oshima is 
located in the south of the Oshima Peninsula.  The area faces Uchiura 
Bay ( Volcano Bay ), Tsugaru Strait, and the Sea of Japan.  #1

The temperature is high compared to other areas in Hokkaidou.  By 
Koeppen's climate classification, the coast of Matsumae Town is 
classified as Cfa ( humid subtropical ), whereas most areas in 
Hokkaidou is classified as Dfb ( humid continental, cool summer ). 

In Hakodate City, the mean annual temperature is 9.1 degrees Celsius 
( average from 1981 to 2010 ), the mean annual precipitation is 1,152 
millimeters, and the mean annual snowfall is 381 centimeters.  #2 

NAME 

The name ``Oshima'' is Japanese.  The pronunciation of the Kanji 
characters ``Watari-shima ( \u6E21\u5CF6 )'' changed to Oshima.  The 
name ``Watari-shima'' means ``across, island.'' 

In the mid 15th century, 12 forts were builts by Japanese people.  
( Forts are called ``tate'' in Japanse. )  During the Edo era, the 
Matsumae Domain ( Matsumae-han ) ruled this area.  The Tokugawa 
Shogunate also ruled this area at one point.  ( The Edo era continued 
from the 17th to the 19th centuries. ) 

( For the former 12 forts, see Kaminokuni Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )
( For the history of the Edo era, see Matsumae Town and Hakodate City 
  in Oshima sub-prefecture. ) 

HISTORY    

During the Meiji period, in 1869 ( Meiji 2 ), Oshima country ( Oshima-
no-kuni ) was established.  The Hokkaidou Development Commission 
( Kaitaku-shi ) was established in Hakodate.  In 1871 ( Meiji 4 ), the 
Hokkaidou Development Commission moved to Sapporo.  The Hakodate's 
building became a branch office.

In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), administrative offices of counties ( ``gun-
yakusho'' ) were established.  Administrative offices of villages and 
towns ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) were established.

The administrative offices opened before this year were a continuation 
of village chiefs ( ``kochou'' ) and town chiefs ( ``kochou'' ) from 
the Edo era. 

In 1882 ( Meiji 15 ), the Hokkaidou Development Commission was 
abolished.  Prefectures of Hakodate, Sapporo, and Nemuro were 
established.  In 1886 ( Meiji 19 ), three prefectures were abolished. 
Hokkaidou Prefecture Office ( Hokkaidou-chou ) was established.

In 1897 ( Meiji 30 ), administrative offices for counties were 
abolished.  Three sub-prefectures ( ``shichou'' ) were established in 
the area what is now Oshihma sub-prefecture.  They were Hakodate, 
Kameda, and Matsumae.

In 1899 ( Meiji 32 ), Hakodate sub-prefecture merged Kameda sub-
prefecture.  In 1903 ( Meiji 36 ), Hakodate sub-prefecture merged 
Matsumae sub-prefecture.  Meiji was a turbulent period.

In 1922 ( Taishou 11 ), Hakodate sub-prefecture was renamed Oshima sub-
prefecture.  In 2010, Oshima General Sub-prefectural Bureau ( Oshima-
sougou-shinkou-kyoku ) was established.

( Oshima-no-kuni = \u6E21\u5CF6\u56FD.  gun-yakusho = 
  \u90E1\u5F79\u6240.  shichou = \u652F\u5E81. )

NOTE

#1

Volcano Bay is called Funka-wan in Japanese.  The name ``Volcano Bay'' 
was named in 1796 by William Robert Broughton.  He was a British naval 
officer.  At the time, volcanoes erupted around the bay.

Some students asked, ``Is Volcano Bay a caldera?''  It's not a 
caldera, although the shape resembles a caldera.  There is no caldera 
as large as this size in the country.  

The largest caldera is Kussharo Caldera in Hokkaidou.  It is 
located in Teshikaga Town in Kushiro sub-prefecture.  When I was in 
elementary school, I heard that the world largest caldera was Aso 
Caldera in Kumamoto Prefecture.  Today, the world largest caldera is 
located in Indonesia.

#2

In Toukyou ( Chiyoda Ward, the representative data of Japan ), the mean 
annual temperature is 15.4 degrees Celsius ( average from 1981 to 
2010 ).  The mean annual rainfall is 1,529 millimeters.  The climate is 
classified as Cfa ( humid subtropical ) by Koeppen's climate 
classification. 

As for the coldest month, the mean temperature of Toukyou is 5.2 
degrees Celsius in January ( average from 1981 to 2010 ).  The mean 
temperature of Hakodate is -2.6 degrees Celsius in January.

As for the hottest month, the mean temperature of Toukyou is 26.4 
degrees Celsius in August.  The mean temperature of Hakodate is 22.0 
degrees Celsius in August. 

In Hokkadou, it's cold after Obon ( 15th August ).  In Toukyou, it's 
still hot after the equinox ( 23rd September, ``Shuubun'' in 
Japanese ).

LOCATION 

Hiyama ( hi-ya-ma ) ( \u6A9C\u5C71 ) is a sub-prefecture ( ``shinkou-
kyoku'' ) in south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  Hiyama is located in the 
west of the Oshima Peninsula.  It faces the Sea of Japan.  The area is 
divided into north and south.  #1

The climate is mild compared to other regions in Hokkaidou.  The warm 
Tsushima Current flowing north influences.  In winter, it's windy due 
to the monsoon that blows from the continent.

According to Koeppen's climate classification, most of the region is 
classified as Dfb ( humid continental, cool summer ).  The coast of 
Esashi Town and the coast of Okushiri island are classified as Cfa 
( humid subtropical ).  The other coasts are classified as Cfb 
( temperate oceanic ).  The population is distributs along the coast.

In Esashi Town, the mean annual temperature is 10.1 degrees Celsius 
( average from 1981 to 2010 ), the mean annual precipitation is 1,197 
millimeters, and the mean snowfall is 330 centimeters.  #2

NAME   

The name ``Hiyama'' is Japanese. ``Hi-yama'' means ``cypress ( sa'i-
pres ), mountain.''  The name was suggested by Takeshirou Matsuura in 
the beginning of the Meiji period.  Takeshirou Matsuura was an explorer 
of Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ) during the Edo era.  #3  

HISTORY 

There used to be natural forests of Hiba trees in Hiyama. ( Hiba is a 
kind of cypress. )  After the late 17th century, during the Edo era, 
the Matsumae Domain felled Hiba trees.  The area was what is now Hiyama 
sub-prefecture.  #4  #5

Japanese immigration has a long history here.  In the mid 15th century, 
the Andou clan built 12 forts.  The area was what are now Hiayama and 
Oshima sub-prefectures.  In the Edo era, the southern part of Hiyama 
was ruled by the Matsumae Domain.  It was also ruled by the Tokugawa 
Shogunate for a time.  

After the Meiji period, in 1869 ( Meiji 2 ), this area was included in 
Oshima country ( Oshima-no-kuni ) and Shiribeshi country ( Shiribeshi-
no-kuni ).  In 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), Esashi branch of the Hokkaidou 
Development Commission was established.

In 1897 ( Meiji 30 ), Hiyama sub-prefecture ( Hiyama-shichou ) was 
established.  In 2010, Hiyama Sub-prefectural Bureau ( Hiyama-shinkou-
kyoku ) was established.  

( Oshima-no-kuni = \u6E21\u5CF6\u56FD.  Shiribeshi-no-kuni = 
  \u5F8C\u5FD7\u56FD.  Hiyama-shichou = \u6A9C\u5C71\u652F\u5E81. )

MUNICIPALITY  

These are municipalities in Hiyama ; ( 1 ) Setana Town 9,590 
( population in 2010 ), ( 2 ) Esashi Town 9,004, ( 3 ) Imakane Town 
6,186, ( 4 ) Kaminokuni Town 5,428, ( 5 ) Assabu Town 4,409, ( 6 ) 
Otobe Town 4,408, and ( 7 ) Okushiri Town 3,033.  The sub-prefectural 
bureau is located in Esashi Town.

The population of Hiyama is the smallest of all the sub-prefectures.  
All municipalities in Hiyama are designated as depopulated areas.  
( Depopulate area is called ``kaso-chiiki'' in Japanese. )

NOTE 
  
#1

Hiyama sub-prefecture is divided into two areas, the northern 
and the southern parts.  In the middle part, there was Kumaishi Town, 
which was merged to Yakumo Town in Oshima sub-prefecture.  

Oshima sub-prefecture is located on the Pacific Ocean side.  Hiyama 
sub-prefecture is located on the Sea of Japal side.  There were many 
reasons for the merger across sub-prefectures. 

#2

In Toukyou ( Chiyoda Ward, the representative data of Japan ), the 
mean annual temperature is 15.4 degrees Celsius ( average from 1981 to 
2010 ).  The mean annual rainfall is 1,529 millimeters.  The climate 
is classified as Cfa ( humid subtropical ) by Koeppen's climate 
classification. 

As for the coldest month, the mean temperature of Toukyou is 5.2 
degrees Celsius in January ( average from 1981-2010 ).  The mean 
temperature of Esashi is -0.8 degrees Celsius in January.
 
As for the hottest month, the mean temperature of Toukyou is 26.4 
degrees Celsius in August.  The mean temperature of Esashi is 22.6 
degrees Celsius in August.
  
#3

Takeshirou Matsuura ( 1818-1888 ) was an explorer of Ezo ( old 
Hokkaidou ) in the Edo era.  He documented place names in Ezo island 
and published the book ``Ezo region county name survey report ( Ezochi-
gunmei-no-gui-torishirabe-sho ).''  

The book was submitted to the Meiji government in 1869 ( Meiji 2 ).  
The government determined place names in Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ) based on 
the book.  The name ``Hiyama'' was the one of them. 

As for the name ``Hiyama,'' Takeshirou Matsuura may have adopted the 
name of the old Hiyama Castle, which was built by the Andou clan in 
their home land. 

Before the Edo era, in the 15th century, the Andou clan migrated to Ezo 
and built 12 forts.  The clan returned to their home land, which was 
located in what is now the Touhoku region.  In their home land, the 
clan built Hiyama Castle, which was located in what is now Akita 
Prefecture. 
	
( For the former 12 forts, see Kaminokuni Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )
( Ezochi-gunmei-no-gui-torishirabe-sho = 
  \u8766\u5937\u5730\u90E1\u540D\u4E4B\u5100\u53D6\u8ABF\u66F8. )
 
#4

The name ``Hi-yama'' means ``cypress mountain.''  In Honshuu island, 
usually, cypress trees are called Hinoki.  In Hokkaidou island and 
Aomori Prefecture, cypress trees are called Hiba.  Hinoki and Hiba look 
alike, but they are different trees.
 
Hou-ryuu-ji Temple ( Buddhist temple ) is the oldest wooden building in 
the country.  It was rebuilt in 670.  The temple is made of Hinoki 
trees.  Temple carpenters ( ``miya-daiku'' ) consider Hinoki to be the 
best wood for construction ( by Nihei lab 2024-2025a ).

Another name of Hiba trees is Hinoki-asunaro.  It means ``tomorrow, it 
will be Hinoki trees'' in Japanese.  This means that the wood of Hiba 
is inferior to Hinoki.  Actually, the wood of Hiba is good too.

The leaves of Hiba are denser than Hinoki.  Hiba grows slower than the 
trees of Hinoki, Sugui ( ceder ), and Todo-matsu ( fir ).  Hiba grows 
even on shady north slopes and moist soils along the coast.  It grows 
slowy and steadily. 

The wood of Hiba has a tight grain and is resistant to moisture.  The 
wood is less likely to warp and less likely to rot.  It was used for 
pillars, bathtubs, boats, bridges, and cutting boards.

The natural forests in Hiyama sub-prefecture were said ``Good trees 
even along the coastline ( Namiuchi-guiwa-yori-ryouzai-shigeri ).''  
The natural forests were disappeared because of felling and fires 
during the Edo era. 

Today, two natural forests are preserved in remote mountains.  They 
have been designated as National Natural Monuments ( Tennen-kinen-
butsu ). 

( For cypress felling, see Assabu Town and Esashi Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )
( For the oldest building in Hokkaidou, see Kaminokuni Town in Hiyama 
  sub-prefecture. )
 
#5

High school teacher Masao Kanamori says ; ``Hiyama's culture is 
different from other parts in Hokkaidou.  You will see it when you 
visit festivals.''

LOCATION 

Matsumae ( ma-tsu-ma-e ) ( \u677E\u524D ) is a town in Oshima sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  Matsumae is located in 
the southwest of the Oshima Peninsula.  The part is called the Matsumae 
Peninsula. 

Matsumae is the southernmost municipality in Hokkaidou.  The area faces 
the Tsugaru Strait and the Sea of Japan.  It includes Ooshima island 
and Kojima island on the Sea of Japan.  #1  #2 

The area is mountainous.  Forests occupy 87 percent.  Rivers are short. 
Mt. Dai-sen-guen ( 1,072 meters ) stands to the north.  There was a 
gold mine in Mt. Dai-sen-guen.

The climate along the coast is mild.  It is classified as ``Cfa'' by 
Koeppen's classification.  ``Cfa'' means ``humid subtropical.''  Almost 
all other parts of Hokkaidou are classified as ``Dfb,'' which means 
``humid continental and cool summer.''   

Camellia trees and Mousou-dake bamboo grow.  Camellia is a forever-
green broad-leaved tree.  Tea and Camellia are from the same family.  
Mousou-dake is a large bamboo.

In Hokkaidou, ``bamboo'' usually means ``bamboo grass.''  Bamboo grass 
is a small bamboo, which is called ``sasa'' in Japanese.  Although 
bamboo grass is small, it is a large organism, because the stems are 
linked by roots.  

( For the gold miners in Mt. Dai-sen-guen, see Fukushima Town in Oshima 
  sub-prefecture. ) 

NAME

The name ``Matsumae'' comes from ``mat-oma-i'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``woman, live, place.''  It signified the uniqueness of the place 
where Japanese women lived.  The etymology is similar to Kuromatsunai 
Town in Shiribeshi sub-prefecture.  ( Ainu people are an indigenous 
ethnic group in Hokkaidou. ) 

In Ezo ( old Hokkaidou ), most Japanese people returned to their 
homelands in winter.  The name ``Matsumae'' signified that the place 
was the territory of Japanese people.  They could have children in the 
place.  They could reproduce. 

HISTORY 

In 720, the name ``Watari-shima-tsugaru-tsu'' was recorded.  It meant a 
port ( ``tsu'' ) between Watari-shima ( now Hokkaidou Prefecture ) and 
Tsugaru ( now Aomori Prefecture ).  It signified what is now the Port 
of Matsumae.

In the 14th century, the name ``Matouma-inu'' was recorded.  From the 
13th to the 15th centuries, the Andou clan came from Tsugaru ( now 
Aomori Prefecture ).  The clan built 12 forts in what is now south 
Hokkaidou.  They traded with Ainu people. 

In 1457, Koshamain's War happened.  Koshamain was a chief of Ainu 
people.  In the war, the Ainu forces destroyed 10 forts out of the 12 
forts.  #3

During the Edo era, Matsumae was the capital of the Matsumae Domain.  
It was the northernmost castle town in Japan.  The Matsumae Domain ran 
major ports in Ezo ( old Hokkaidou ) ; Matsumae, Esashi, and Hakodate. 
The ports were gateways to Ezo.  ``Matsmai'' in Moby Dick ( a novel ) 
means Hokkaidou.

At the end of the Edo era, in 1868, the Matsumae Domain separated from 
the Tokugawa Shogunate.  They supported the new Meiji government.  In 
the beginning of the Meiji period, the Boshin War happened.  The former 
Tokugawa Shogunate forces landed Ezo and captured the Port of Hakodate 
and Fukuyama Castle ( Matsumae Castle ). 

( For the former 12 forts and Koshamain's War, see Kaminokuni Town in 
  Hiyama sub-prefecture. )

  Matouma-inu = \u4E07\u5802\u5B87\u6E80\u4F0A\u72AC. )

In 1869 ( Meiji 2 ), the Meiji government forces recaptured Fukuyama 
Castle.  They advanced to the Port of Hakodate, defeated the 
former Tokugawa Shogunate, and ended the Boshin War.  After the war, 
the Matsumae Domain was renamed the Tate Domain.  It was named after 
Tate Castle in what is now Assabu Town.

In 1871 ( Meiji 4 ), the Tate Domain was abolished and Aomori 
Prefecture governed the south part of Hokkaidou.  In 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), 
Matsumae branch of the Hokkaidou Development Commission ( Kaitaku-shi ) 
was established.

In 1881 ( Meiji 14 ), the administrative office of Matsumae county 
( Matsumae-gun-yakusho ) was established.  Matsumae branch of the 
Hokkaidou Development Commission was abolished.  In 1897 ( Meiji 
30 ), Matsumae sub-prefecture was established.  The administrative 
office of Matsumae county was abolished. 

In 1900 ( Meiji 33 ), Fukuyama Town ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established.  At the time, 34 towns in the built-up area of Matsumae 
were merged.  Those were small towns that had existed in the Edo era.  
#4 

In 1903 ( Meiji 36 ), Hakodate sub-prefecture merged Matsumae sub-
prefecture.  In 1915 ( Taishou 4 ), Ooshima Village ( 2nd-class 
municipality ) was established by the merger of three villages ; 
Eramachi, Haraguchi, and Kiyobe.  ( Ooshima village was not Ooshima 
island. ) 

In 1923 ( Taishou 12 ), Kojima Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of five villages ; Nebuta, Akagami, 
Satsumae, Mogusa, and Utari.  ( Kojima village was not Kojima island. )

In the same year, Oosawa Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of four villages ; Oosawa, Araya, Sumiyaki-
zawa, and Kami-oyobe.  In 1940 ( Shouwa 15 ), Fukuyama Town was renamed 
Matsumae Town.  In 1954 ( Shouwa 29 ), Matsumae Town merged the 
villages of Ooshima, Kojima, and Oosawa.  #5

TOWN HALL, TRAFFIC

The town hall of Matsumae and Matsumae Castle ( Fukuyama Castle ) are 
located near the mouth of the Oomatsumae River.  National Route 228 
( Fukuyama-kaidou or Matsumae-kokudou ) passes there.  ``Fukuyama-
kaidou'' was an old trail that connected Matsumae and Hakodate in the 
Edo era.  #6  

In the past, the Matsumae Line run.  The railroad connected Kikonai 
Town and Matsumae Town.  The terminus was Matsumae Station.  The 
population of Matsumae peaked at 20,072 in 1955.  #7

( For Fukuyama-kaidou, see Fukushima Town in Oshima sub-prefecture. )

NOTE 
 
#1

The southernmost of Hokkaidou island is Cape Shirakami.  It is located 
along National Route 228 in Matsumae Town.  The latitude is 41 degrees 
23.87 minutes north.  The longitude is 140 degrees 11.86 minutes east. 

If small islands are counted, the southernmost of Hokkaidou Prefecture 
is Kojima island.  Kojima island is also called Matsumae-kojima or 
Oshima-kojima.  It's an uninhabited island. 

Ooshima island is the largest uninhabited island in the country.  The 
area is 9.7 square kilometers. Ooshima island is also called Matsumae-
ooshima or Oshima-ooshima. 

Ooshima island is a volcano.  It erupted in 1741 and caused tidal 
waves.  The tidal waves killed 1,467 people in the Matsumae Domain and 
566 people in the Tsugaru Domain.  ( In the Edo era, the Matsumae 
Domain ruled the south of Hokkaidou Prefecture.  The Tsugaru Domain 
ruled the west of Aomori Prefecture. ) 

Ordinary tourists cannot land Ooshima island and Kojima island.  Only 
shelters for fishing boats exist in these islands.  These islands are 
breeding grounds of seabirds such as murre ( mi'-ar ) ( ``umi-
garasu'' ) and streaked shearwater ( ``oo-mizunagui-dori'' ).

#3

In 1457, the warlord Nobuhiro Takeda ended Koshamain's War.  Nobuhiro 
Takeda was a vassal of the Andou clan.  Then, he inherited the Kakizaki 
clan, which was also a vassal of the Andou clan.  Around 1549, his 
great-grandson, Suehiro Kakizaki, made peace with Ainu chiefs.  

In 1593, Yoshihiro Kakizaki, son of Suehiro Kakizaki, became 
independent from the Andou clan.  He was able to ask Hideyoshi Toyotomi 
for the land of Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ).  Hideyoshi Toyotomi was the 
ruler during the Warring State era. 

The meeting between Hideyoshi Toyotomi and Yoshihiro Kakizaki was 
arranged by the Maeda clan.  The Maeda clan was the ruler of the Kaga 
Domain ( now Ishikawa Prefecture ).

In 1598, Hideyoshi Toyotomi died.  In 1599, Yoshihiro Kakizaki gave 
maps of Ezo to Ieyasu Tokugawa, who would become the founder of the 
Tokugawa Shogunate.  Maps were important for governing lands during the 
Warring States era.  Giving maps meant obedience.
 
In 1604, at the beginning of the Edo era, the Tokugawa Shogunate 
appointed Yoshihiro Kakizaki as the lord of the Matsumae Domain.  
Yoshihiro Kakizaki changed his family name from Kakizaki to Matsumae. 

There is a legend that the name ``Matsumae'' comes from Matsudaira and 
the Maeda clan ( Matsu + Mae ).  ``Matsudaira'' was Ieyasu Tokugawa's 
old family name. 

( For Nobuhiro Takeda, see Kaminokuni Town in Hiyama sub-prefecture. )

#6

Fukuyama Castle is the former name of Matsumae Castle.  Fukuyama Castle

1949.  Currently, the only national treasure in Hokkaidou is a clay 
figurine, which was dug out from a potato field.

( For national treasure, see Hakodate City in Oshima sub-prefecture. )
 
#7

Matsumae Town is a sister city with Oumi-hachiman City in Shiga 
Prefecture and Yanagawa Town ( now Date City ) in Fukushima Prefecture. 
The reasons date back to the Edo era as follows.

Merchants from Oumi-hachiman were called Hachiman-shounin.  They traded 
in Ezo using Kitamae-bune ( North-bound ship ).  They based in the 
castle town of Matsumae.  They developed herring fishing grounds.  
( Hachiman-shounin was included in Oumi-merchants ( Oumi-shounin ). )

Regarding Yanagawa, in 1807, the Tokugawa Shogunate directly ruled Ezo 
to prevent Russian southward movement.  The Tokugawa Shogunate judged 
that the Mastumae Domain's behavior towards Russians was bad. 

The vassals of Matsumae Domain were forced to moved to Yanagawa.  Half 
of the vassals became ``rounin'' ( jobless samurai ).  They lived in 
hardship for 14 years until the Matsumae Domain was returned to Ezo in 
1821. 

#8

Dried flying squid is called ``surume'' in Japanese.  Matsumae Town 
and neighboring Fukushima Town are the largest producers of dried 
flying squid in the country.

The tonnages of dried flying squid from the two towns account for 80 
percent of the national share.  In the late Meiji period ( about 100 
years ago ), herring fishing decreased and flying squid fishing 
prospered.

In the past, fishers processed flying squid into ``surume'' ( dried 
flying squid ).  As catches increased, after the 1970s, local companies 
started processing flying squid ( by Matsumae Town's website ``History 
of Matsumae Surume.''  Accessed in May 2019 ).

Matsumae-zuke is pickles made with Konbu kelp, dried flying squid, and 
herring roe.  A variety of Matsumae-zuke are sold at the souvenir shop 
``Dosanko Plaza'' in Sapporo Station.  In the past, when herring 
catches were poor, Matsumae-zuke without herring roe was sold.

One of the oldest Somei-yoshino trees in the country grows at Matsumae 
Castle.  It was planted in 1882 ( Meiji 15 ).  Somei-yoshino is the 
most famous cherry variety in the country.  It was bred in Somei 
village near Edo ( now central Toukyou ) in the end of the Edo era.  
Somei village was located near present-day Ikebukuro.

Somei-yoshino was named after Mt. Yoshino, a famous cherry blossom site 
in Nara Prefecture.  The cherries in Mt. Yoshino are mountain cherries 
( not Somei-yoshino ). 

Somei-yoshino trees are clones.  If the soil and climate are the same, 
they grow into the same shape.  If you observe a row of the cherry 
trees, you'll find similarities in their height, trunk diameter, trunk 
twist, and flowering time.  Somei-yoshino trees are used as an 
indicator for the season of cherry blossom. 

Making plant clones are easier than animal clones.  Clones are more 
alike than siblings.  Plant clones are the predecessors of today's

LOCATION    

Fukushima ( fu-ku-shi-ma ) ( \u798F\u5CF6 ) is a town in Oshima sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  The area faces the 
Tsugaru Strait to the south.  The Fukushima River and the Shiriuchi 
River flow.

Forests cover 93 percent of the area.  Mt. Dai-sen-guen ( 1,072 
meters ) rises to the north.  Matsumae county consists of Matsumae Town 
and Fukushima Town and is located at the southern end of Hokkaidou.

NAME 

The name ``Fukushima'' is Japanese.  ``Fuku-shima'' means ``lucky, 
island.''  It's a good-luck name given by an oracle from Tsukizaki 
Shrine in 1624.  #1  #2 

HISTORY 

The first Japanese immigrants to Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ) came from Ooshuu 
( now Touhoku region ) in 1189.  They were vassals of the Fujiwara 
clan, which were defeated by Yoritomo Minamoto.  They lived in what is 
now Yoshioka district, Fukushima Town.  ( Yoritomo Minamoto established 
the Kamakura Shogunate after he conquered Ooshuu. )  
 
In the mid 15th century, there was a fort whose name was Onnai-date in 
Yoshioka district.  The fort was destroyed by the Ainu forces during 
Koshamain's War.  In the Edo era, this area was ruled by the Matsumae 
Domain. Around 1617, a gold mine opened in Mt. Dai-sen-guen.  The gold 
miners were Catholic.  #3 
 
In the beginning of the Meiji period, the Matsumae domain was renamed 
the Tate Domain.  The area that is now Fukushima Town belonged to the 
Tate Domain.  

In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), two administrative offices ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) 
were established ; one was for Fukushima village and Shirafu village ; 
the other was for Yoshioka village, Reihige village, and Miyanouta 
village.

In 1900 ( Meiji 33 ), Fukushima Village ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Fukushima village and Shirafu village.  In 
1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Yoshioka Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Yoshioka, Reihige, and Miyanouta villages. 

In 1944 ( Shouwa 19 ), Fukushima Town was established.  In 1955 
( Shouwa 30 ), Fukushima Town and Yoshioka Village were merged.

NOTE  

#1

The book ``Fukushima Town history ( Fukushima-chou-shi ), volume 2'' 
published in 1995 states the etymology of Fukushima as follows ; 
``Once upon a time, Orikanai village was on the verge of being 
abandoned, because of a large fire and poor fishing catches.  

( continue ) --> At the time, there was an oracle from Tsukisaki 
Shrine, which said, `The name Orikanai must be changed to Fukushima.'  
The name `Fukushima' may have referred to Fukuyama.'' 
 
Fukuyama was the castle town on of the Matsumae Domain ( Now Matsumae 
Town. )  Matsumae and Fukushima are neighbouring municipalities.  The 
name ``Fuku-shima'' means ``lucky, island'' and ``Fuku-yama'' means 
``lucky, mountain.''   

The original name of Fukushima was ``Orikanai.''  It comes from 
``horka-nay'' in Ainu language, which means ``reverse, river.''  It 
signified the Fukushima River.  Seawater entered the estuary at high 
tide, or the river mouth meandered in a U-shape.  The etymology is the 
same as Horokanai Town in Kamikawa sub-prefecture. 

#2

These are old Shintou shrines in Fukushima Town.  ( 1 ) Tsukisaki 
Shrine, reconstructed in 1492, ( 2 ) Fukushima Daijinguu Shrine, 
reconstructed in 1573, ( 3 ) Shirafu Daijinguu Shrine, established in 
1655 by the Matsumae Domain, and ( 4 ) Kamaya Shrine, established in 
1772. 

Shirafu Daijinguu Shirine preserves the traditional dance ``Shirafu-
arauma-odori ( Shirafu, wild horse, dance ).''  The dance represented 
the power of the Matsumae Domain.  It has been designated as National 
Intangible Cultural Property ( Mukei-bunka-zai ).

Kamaya Shrine is located in Shiogama district.  The name ``Shio-gama'' 
is Japanese, which means ``salt, pot.''  It was derived from the 
production of salt for sardine fishing.

#3

The mountain's name ``Dai-sen-guen'' is Japanese.  It means ``large, 
thousand, houses.''  It signified that there were 1,000 houses of gold 
miners.  During the Edo era, the Tokugawa Shogunate banned 
Christianity, but the regulation was loose in Ezo island ( now 
Hokkaidou ), because the island was seen as an outer land 
( ``gaichi'' ). 

In 1637, Christians revolted in Kyuushuu island.  In 1939, Christians 
in Ezo island were executed.  At the site of the gold mine in Mt. Dai-
sen-guen, there is an inscription that reads ``Christian martyrdom 
( ma'r-ta-dam ).''

( For gold, see Naka-tonbetsu Town in Souya sub-prefecture. )

is rare.  But, white birch ( ``Shira-kanba'' ) and alder ( ``Han-no-
ki'' ) cause pollen fever, although the effects are weaker than cedar.

I didn't hear about pollen fever when I was a child.  It was a good 
time.  ( I caught pollen fever after I moved to Toukyou.  When I remove 
the motorbike cover, yellow dust ( pollen ) rises up like smoke.  )

In the past, cedar trees were small.  In addition, people may have 
developed a tolerance for dust and pollen, because many people smoked. 
 
Cedar trees grow even on wet land.  Farmers in Honshuu island planted 
cedar trees on abandoned rice paddies near valley heads.  During the 
high economic growth period, from the late 1960s to the early 1970s, 
farm successors left the countryside.  Land use was changed from 
agricultural to forest.  Taxes were reduced.

LOCATION 

Shiriuchi ( shi-ri-u-chi ) ( \u77E5\u5185 ) is a town in Oshima sub-

Forests cover 87 percent of the area.

NAME    

The name ``Shiriuchi`` comes from ``cir-ot-i'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``bird, exist, place.''  It signified the Shiriuchi River.  In 
the Edo era, Shiriuchi was a hunting ground for hawks and falcons.  
Young hawks and falcons were caught and shipped to Edo ( now central 
Toukyou ) for falconry.  #1

HISTORY

In 1205, the feudal lord of Kai Province ( now Yamanashi Prefecture ) 
started gold dust gathering along the Shiriuchi River.  It was an order 
from the Kamakura Shogunate.

In the 15th century, there was a fort called Wakimoto-date ( da'-te ).  
It was one of the 12 forts built by the Andou clan, who came from 
Tsugaru ( now Aomori Prefecture ).  During the Edo era, the area what 
is now Shiriuchi was ruled by the Matsumae Domain.  #2

After the Meiji period, in 1871 ( Meiji 4 ), the administrative office 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) of Shiriuchi village was established.  It was a 
continuation of village chiefs from the Edo era.

In 1881 ( Meiji 14 ), the administrative office ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) 
of Shiriuchi village was re-established.  It covered Kotaniishi village 
( by ``New Shiriuchi Town history ( Shin-Shiriuchi-choushi )'' 
published in 2015 ). 

In 1906 ( Meiji 39 ), Shiriuchi Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Shiriuchi village and Kotaniishi village.  
In 1967 ( Shouwa 42 ), Shiriuchi Town was established.

#1

In the Edo era, the Matsumae Domain traded hawks and falcons.  ( In 
Japanese, hawk is ``taka'' and falcon is ``hayabusa.'' )  There were 
many bird-catching places in Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ).  Shiriuchi was one 
of the best places. 

Hawk catchers ( ``taka-machi'' ) searched for the nests of hawk and 
falcon nests in summer.  They wintered in Ezo, caught hawk chicks in 
spring, and handed them to the Matsumae Domain.

Young hawks and falcons were shipped to Edo ( now central Toukyou ).  
The Tokugawa Shogunate trained them for falconry.  Feathers of hawks 
were also traded for making arrow fletchings. 

Ainu people also caught hawks and falcons.  They hid in huts nearby 
streams.  Using salmon as lure, Ainu hunters caught the birds by 
hooking their legs.  The birds were sold to Japanese people.  

Ainu people kept hawks to take feathers.  They kept young bears too.  
Ainu people called bears ``kamuy,'' which means ``god.''  Today, 
Japanese people keep bears.

In 1871 ( Meiji 4 ), Kanrin-maru ( a ship ) sank at Cape Saraki in

Kanrin-maru was a battleship, which the Tokugawa Shogunate purchased 
from the Netherlands.  It was the first Japanese ship that crossed the 
Pacific Ocean.  In 1860, to complete the conclusion of the Japan-US 
Treaty of Amity and Commerce, Kanrin-maru departed from the Port of

Kanrin-maru arrived in the Port of San Francisco on February 26th.  The 
captain was Kaishuu Katsu and the interpreter was Manjirou John.  On 
the outbound voyage, bad weather caused Japanese crew to be sea-sick.  
Americans took over piloting.

In 1867, the steam of Kanrin-maru was removed.  It became a cargo ship. 
After the Meiji period, the new Meiji government owned the ship.  It 
sank during a heavy storm in September 1871, off the coast of Kikonai. 

At the time, Kanrin-maru carried 401 immigrants from Shiroishi Castle 
in the Sendai Domain ( now Miyagui Prefecture ) to Hokkaidou.  No one 
died in the the shipwreck.

#4

The former Oono Town is the birth place of paddy rice cultivation in 
Hokkaidou.  It started in the Edo era.  There is a stone monument that 
commemorates the first paddy rice.  The inscription reads ; 

``In 1692, a farmer Sakuemon opened a paddy filed of 450 tsubo ( 0.15 
hectares ) using natural water and gained rice of 10-hyou.''  ( One 
``hyou'' of rice is 60 kg. )
 
The record was excellent at the time. Today, a good paddy field has a 
yield of 600 kg per 0.1 hectares.  Such paddy field is called ``jippyo-
dori ( 10-hyou gaining paddy ).''

According to oral tradition, paddy rice cultivation in the former Oono 
Town dates back to the Kanbun period ( 1661-1672 ) and 1685 ( by 
Yoshiaki Ishizuka & Tatsuzou Hoshino eds. 1994.  ``Hokkaidou-no-inasaku 
( Rice cultivation of Hokkaidou ).''  Hokunoukai. )

Kametarou Ootomo ( 1834-1897 ) was an engineer in the Edo era.  He 
instructed agriculture in Oono village ( now Hokuto City ).  In 1866, 
at the end of the Edo era, he moved to Sapporo with villagers and 
started developing.

Kametarou Ootomo constructed the Ootomo Canal.  It is now called the 
Sousei River and flows through the center of Sapporo City.  Kametarou 
Ootomo was a disciple of Sontoku Ninomiya ( 1787-1856 ).

Kyuuzou Nakayama was the owner of a post station ( ``eki-tei'' ) in 
what is now Kita-hirosima City.  He was known as the father of cold-
climate paddy rice cultivation.  In the Meiji period, he obtained rice 
seeds in Oono village, and succeeded in paddy rice cultivation in the 
Ishikari Plain.

LOCATION 

Hakodate ( ha-ko-da-te ) ( \u51FD\u9928 ) is a city in Oshima sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  The area faces Hakodate 
Bay, Tsugaru Strait, and the Pacific Ocean.  It occupies the southeast 
of the Oshima Peninsula.  This part is called the Kameda Peninsula. 

Mt. Hakodate stands from Hakodate Bay.  The height is 334 meters.  
( Toukyou Tower is 333 meters. )  The area of Hakodate was newly formed 
in 2004 by the merger of Hakodate City, Toi Town, Minami-kayabe Town, 
Esan Town, and Todohokke Village.  #1  #2  #3 

NAME

The name ``Hako-date'' means ``box, fort'' in Japanese.  It referred to 
a fortress, whose shape resembled a box.  The name of the fort was 
Usukeshi-date.  It was managed by the Kouno clan in the 1450s.  The 
Kouno clan was one of the vassals of the Andou clan, who built 12 forts 
in what is now south Hokkaidou.

HISTORY   

Koshamain's War occurred in 1457.  Koshamain was a chief of Ainu 
people.  During the war, Usukeshi-date, so-called Hako-date, was 
destroyed by the Ainu forces.

In the Edo era, the Port of Hakodate was managed by the Matsumae Domain 
and later by the Tokugawa Shogunate.  The port opened for American 
ships in 1854 under the Convention of Kanagawa ( Japan-US Treaty of 
Peace and Amity ).  #4

In the beginning of the Meiji period, Hakodate became the battle ground 
of the Boshin War ( 1868-1869 ).  In 1869 ( Meiji 2 ), the Hokkaidou 
Development Commission was established.  In 1871, the head office of 
the Hokkaidou Development Commission moved to Sapporo.  #5

In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), Hakodate Ward ( Hakodate-ku ) was established.  
This ``ward'' was an administrative district and a municipality.  ( A 
municipality has a local government. )  In 1899 ( Meiji 32 ), Hakodate 
Ward ( Hakodate-ku ) was established.  This ``Ward'' was a 
municipality, which was designated especially in Hokkaidou.

In 1922 ( Taishou 11 ), Hakodate City was established.  In 1939 
( Shouwa 14 ), Hakodate City merged Yunokawa Town ( 1st-class 
municipality ).  Yunokawa Town was located around Yunokawa Hot Spring.

In 1966 ( Shouwa 41 ), Hakodate City merged Zenigame-zawa Village ( the 
former 1st-class municipality ).  Zenigame-zawa Village was located 
near Hakodate Airport.  In 1973 ( Shouwa 48 ), Hakodate City merged 
Kameda City.  #6

( For the Andou clan and Koshamain's War, see Kaminokuni Town in Hiyama 
  sub-prefecture. ) 
( For Ward  ( ``ku'' ) in Hokkaidou, see Kushiro Town in Kushiro sub-

CITY HALL, TRAFFIC, POPULATION
  
The city hall of Hakodate and the Port of Hakodate are located at the 
foot of Mt. Hakodate.  National Route 5 and the Hakodate Main Line 
start from the port.

Hakodate was the gateway to Japan in the late 19th century, during the 
Edo era.  Hakodate was the gateway to Hokkaidou from the late 19th 
century to the late 20th century.

The Seikan train ferry ( Seikan-renraku-sen ) connected Hakodate and 
Aomori.  The name ``Sei-kan'' is an abbreviation of Aomori and 
Hakodate in Kanji characters.  #7

The population peaked in 1980 at 345,165 ( including municipalities of 
Hakodate, Toi, Esan, Minami-kayabe, and Todohokke ).  The population 
decreased, because of ; ( 1 ) end of pelagic fishing ( deep-sea 
fishing ), ( 2 ) opening of the Seikan Tunnel, ( 3 ) increase in air 
travelers, and ( 4 ) motorization and suburbanization.  #8

#4

The Port of Hakodate was a natural port.  Japanese people called it 
``tsuna-shirazu ( rope, no need ).''  They also called it Tomoe Port 
( spiral port ) due to the spiral-like topography.

The Japan-US Treaty of Peace and Amity ( Nichibei-washin-jouyaku ) was 
concluded in 1854.  It was concluded between the Tokugawa Shogunate and 
the United States of America.  The ports of Hakodate and Shimoda were 
opened.  ( Shimoda is located in the Izu Peninsula, Shizuoka 
Prefecture. )

( The name ``Japan'' is English.  In Japanese, it is called ''Nihon.''  
  So, the name ``the Sea of Japan'' is 100 percent English. )  

Before the treaty was concluded, the commander of the East India 
Squadron, Matthew Perry, had appeared in many ports in Japan.  Japanese 
people were surprised to see the American ships.  They moved by steam 
engine.  ( Japanese ships were moved by the wind. )  The flagship was 
Susquehanna.  It was called the black ship ( ``kuro-fune'' ).
 
( For steamers, see Takikawa City in Sorachi sub-prefecture. )

In 1858, the Ansei Five-Power Treaties ( Ansei-gokakoku-jouyaku ) was 
signed between Japan (the Tokugawa Shogunate), the USA, the UK, Russia, 
the Netherlands, and France.  The most influential of the treaties was 
the Treaty of Amity and Commerce Between the United States and Japan 
( Nichibei-shuukou-tsuushou-jouyaku).

Then, along with Hakodate, the ports of Kanagawa ( now Yokohama ), 
Nagasaki, Hyougo ( now Koube ), and Niigata were opened.  The Port of 
Shimoda was closed instead of Kanagawa.  Foreign settlements were built 
near the ports.  Cities of Edo ( now central Toukyou ) and Oosaka were 
opened for foreign visitors.  

Thus, Japanese isolationist policy was ended, due to the effort 
( pressure ) of Matthew Perry.  The isolationist policy continued for 
250 years.  It was a period that resembled a perfect closed circle.

#5

The Boshin War was a civil war between the new Meiji government forces 
and the former Tokugawa Shogunate forces.  The final phase is called 
the Hakodate War.  The former Tokugawa Shogunate forces aimed to 
establish the Republic of Ezo.  ( Ezo is what is now Hokkaidou. ) 

( For the Boshin War, see Kikonai Town, Matsumae Town, Mori Town in 
  Oshima sub-prefecture, Assabu Town, Esashi Town, and Otobe Town in 
  Hiyama sub-prefecture. )
 
Takeaki Enomoto ( 1836-1908 ) was the commander of the former Tokugawa 
Shogunate forces.  He learned English from John Manjirou and studied in 
the Netherlands.  After the defeat in the Boshin War, Takeaki Enomoto 
was appointed by Kiyotaka Kuroda and worked for the Hokkaidou 
Development Commission. 

Kiyotaka Kuroda ( 1840-1900 ) was a samurai in the Satsuma Domain ( now 
Kagoshima Prefecture. )  He served as an officer in the new Meiji 
government forces.  After the war, he became the undersecretary ( vice 
minister ) of the Hokkaidou Development Commission.  He invited 
American advisors to Hokkaidou.

Toshizou Hijikata ( 1835-1869 ) was an officer of the former Tokugawa 
Shogunate forces.  He was killed in the Hakodate War.  In the Edo era, 
he was the vice-commander of Shinsen-gumi.  Shinsen-gumi was the 
special forces of the Tokugawa Shogunate.
 
Shinpachi Nagakura ( 1839-1915 ) was the strongest swordsman in 
Shinsen-gumi.  There is a small plaque to him near the main gate of 
Hokkaidou University.  He was born and raised in the Edo branch of the 
Matsumae Domain.  ( Edo was the center of today's Toukyou.  The 
Matsumae Domain ruled what is now south Hokkaidou in the Edo era. ) 

Shinpachi Nagakura learned several swordsmanship schools in Edo.  Then, 
he fled the Matsumae Domain and joined Shinsen-gumi.  After Shinsen-
gumi was abolished, he participated in the Boshin War as a member of 
the Tokugawa Shogunate forces.

He didn't participated in the Hakodate War.  ( The Hakodate War was the 
final phase of the Boshin War. )  If he had participated in the 
Hakodate War, he would have to fight against the Matsumae Domain, which 
was his former monarch.

In the Meiji period, Shinpachi Nagakura taught swordsmanship in Kabato 
Prison and in Agricultural College of Touhoku Imperial University ( now 
Hokkaidou University ).

Of all the swordsmanship schools that Shinpachi Nagakura learned, he 
got the highest rank ( ``menkyo-kaiden'' ) in Shintou-munen-ryuu.  
Shintou-munen-ryuu features powerful sword strokes. 

( For Shinpachi Nagakura, see Tsukigata Town in Sorachi sub-
  prefecture. ) 
( For Takeaki Enomoto, see Utashinai City in Sorachi sub-prefecture. )

The Seikan train ferry operated from 1908 ( Meiji 41 ) to 1988 ( Shouwa

There is a famous song called Tsugaru-kaikyou-fuyu-gueshiki ( Tsugaru 
Strait Winter Scene ).  The lyrics depict the people on the Sikan train 
ferry.  These are like this ; ``People returning north are quiet.  They 
only hear the sound of the sea.''
 
This was my sales pitch to invite master students ; ``It's good to live

NOTE
 
#1

As for Nanae Governmental Farm, in 1863, the Gaertner brothers rented 
the land and started Western-style farming.  They came from Prussia 
( now Germany ).  The farm area was 990 hectares.  ( Cf. Hokkaidou 
University Ranch in Hidaka sub-prefecture is 470 hectares. )  

The Gaertner brothers primarily engaged in trade and consulship.  They 
also cultivated apples, pears, gooseberries, cherries, and vegetables.  
The apples were the first Western varieties cultivated in Japan.  The 
Gaertoner brothers taught Western-style farming to neighboring farmers.

They sold food to foreign ships in the Port of Hakodate.  There are no 
photographs or paintings of them.  They planted beech trees.  The trees 
became a forest along National Route 5.  Beech trees were used for 
grazing pigs in their home country.  Beech trees have acorns.  The meat 
of a wild boar ( ``inoshishi'' ) that ate acorns is also good.

In 1870 ( Meiji 3 ), the Gaertner brothers' farm was closed.  The Meiji 
government bought the land back.  On the former site, Nanae Government 
Farm ( Nanae-kan-en ) opened.  

To introduce Western-style farming to Hokkaidou, the Meiji government 
established five government farms in Toukyou and Hokkaidou.  The 
locations were Aoyama and Azabu in Toukyou, and Nanae, Sapporo, and 
Nemuro in Hokkaidou.  Nanae Government Farm was the largest.

An English writer Isabella Lucy Bird visited Nanae Government Farm in 
1878.  She reported the scenery like this ; ``The farm looked like a 
Western country.  It had Western-style buildings such as animal 
stables, a large watermill, and a butter factory.''

In 1882, the Hokkaidou Development Commission was abolished.  Nanae 
Government Farm was gradually sold off and closed in 1894.  Only its 
stone wall remains near Nanae Elementary School.

LOCATION 

Mori ( mo-ri ) ( \u68EE ) is a town in Oshima sub-prefecture, south 
Hokkaidou ( Dou-nan ).  The area faces Uchiura Bay ( Volcano Bay ).  
The Nigorikawa River, the Torizaki River, and the Oshironai River flow. 
 
Mt. Komagatake ( 1,131 meters, volcano ) rises to the south.  The area 
of Mori was newly established by the merger of Mori Town and Sawara 
Town in 2005.  #2

NAME 

The name ``Mori'' means ``forest'' in Japanese.  The original name was 
Oniushi.  The name ``Oniushi'' comes from ``o-ni-us-pet'' in Ainu 
language.  It means ``river mouth, tree, many, river.''  It signified 
the Torizaki River or the Mori River.

The pronunciation of Oni-ushi sounds like ``demon, ox'' in Japanese.  
Japanese immigrants may not have liked the name.

HISTORY 

In the 16th century, the first Japanese immigrants came from Tsugaru 
( now Aomori Prefecture ).  In the Edo era, Kayabe-basho ( trading 
place ) was established.  It was for herring fishing and was managed by 
the Matsumae Domain.  Nigorikawa Hot Spring opened in 1805 in 
Nigorikawa Caldera.  #3   

In the Edo era, villages of Washinoki, Mori, and Oshironai were 
established.  The following is the history after the Meiji period ;

OLD MORI TOWN ; In 1880 ( Meiji 13 ), an administrative office 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) was established in Washinoki village.  It covered 
villages of Ebiya and Ishikura.

In 1881 ( Meiji 14 ), the administrative office of Mori village was 
established.  It covered villages of Oshironai and Shukunobe.  In 1899 
( Meiji 22 ), Mori village merged Washinoki village.  The 
administrative office covered six villages ; Mori, Oshironai, 
Shukunobe, Washinoki, Ebiya, and Ishikura.  #4

In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Mori Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established.  In 1903 ( Meiji 36 ), Mori Station on Hokkaidou Railway 
( now Hakodate Main Line ) opened.  In 1907 ( Meiji 40 ), Mori Village 
( 1st-class municipality ) was established.  In 1921 ( Taishou 10 ), 
Mori Town was established.

( For 1st-class and 2nd-class municipalities see the note of Matsumae 
  Town. )
( Washinoki = \u9DF2\u30CE\u6728.  Ebiya = \u86EF\u8C37.  Ishikura = 
  \u77F3\u5009.  Oshironai = \u5C3E\u767D\u5185.  Shukunobe = 
  \u5BBF\u91CE\u8FBA. )

OLD SAWARA TOWN ; In 1880 ( Meiji 13 ), the administrative office 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) of Sawara village was established.  It covered 
Kakeruma village.  In 1906 ( Meiji 39 ), Sawara Village ( 2nd-class 
municipality ) was established.

In 1927 ( Shouwa 2 ), Sawara Station on Oshima-kaigan-tetsudou ( Oshima 
Coastal Railway ) opened.  It was the current Sawara branch route of 
the Hakodate Main Line.  In 1970 ( Shouwa 45 ), Sawara Town was 
established.

( Sawara = \u7802\u539F.  Kakeruma = \u639B\u6F97. )

Mori River.  National Route 278 ( Esan-kokudou ) branches off from 
National Route 5 ( Oonuma-kokudou ) near the town hall.  National Route 
278 runs along Uchiura Bay.  #5

Mori Station on the Hakodate Main Line is the junction of Oonuma main 
route and Sawara branch route.  Heavy freight ( fre'it ) trains go the 
branch route along the bay.  #6

NOTE

#1

Mori Town belongs to Kayabe county ( Kayabe-gun ).  The name ``Kayabe 
( \u8305\u90E8 )'' comes from ``kaya-un-pe'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``sail, exist, place.''  It signified Mt. Komagatake.  Ainu 
people viewed the shape of the mountain as a sailing ship.

Komagatake Hazard Map was published in 1983.  It was the first volcanic
hazard map in the country.  The map included the towns of Mori, 
Shikabe, and Nanae.  Today, each municipality publshes own hazard map.

Who makes hazard maps?  The answer is consulting companies.  They are 
the main employers of graduates from geological and geographical 
faculties ( by Kazutaka Mannen 2020.  ``Volcanoes as seen from cutting-
edge science.''  Bestbook ( written in Japanese ) ).  Many of my 
classmates work for consulting companies. 

( For Mt. Komagatake, see Nanae Town in Oshima sub-prefecture. )

#2

The pronunciation of ``Mori Town'' in Japanese is ``Mori-machi.''  All 
other towns in Hokkaidou are pronounced ``-chou,'' for example, Kayabe-
chou, Yakumo-chou, and Oshamanbe-chou.

The municipality decides the pronunciation.  It is said that ``-machi'' 
is located mainly in eastern Japan and ``-chou'' is located mainly in 
western Japan.  According to the Chuu-nichi Shinbun ( 2021.7.19 ), the 
town hall of Mori surveyed old telegrams and old gazetteers, and 
decided the name ``Mori-machi'' in 1987.

( Cf. The former Ishikari 10 towns in Ishikari City are mixture of 
  ``-machi'' and ``-chou.''  See Ishikari City in Ishikari sub-
  prefecture. )

The river name ``Torizaki'' comes from ``torii ( shrine gate )'' in 
Japanese.  There was a Shintou shrine in the forest.  It had many Torii 
gates.  The place was called Torii-ga-saki, which means ``shrine gate, 
of, cape'' ( by Kenji Kimura 1798.  ``Ezo-nikki ( Ezo diary )'' ).

On the map published by Takeshirou Matsuura in 1859 ( http://
koukita.github.io/touzaiezo/map/ ), the river is written as Torisaki-
kawa.  It's an old name.

( For Japanese river names, see Setana Town in Hiyama sub-prefecture. )
   
#3

The first immigrants came to Sawara ( now Mori Town ) from Kanita in 
1532.  Kanita was located in what is now Sotogahama Town in Aomori 
Prefecture  ( by ``Sawara Town history ( Sawara-chou-shi )'' published 
in 2000 ). 

The former Sawara Town has a long history of Japanese immigrants, so 
the origin of the name is unclear.  These are estimations from the book 
``Sawara Town history'' ;
( 1 ) The name `` Sawara '' comes from ``sar'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``reed.''  Reed ( plant ) is called ``ashi'' in Japanese.

( 2 ) The name comes from ``sarki'' in Ainu language.  It means ``reed, 
stem.''  This may signify that the reed is long or stiff.

( 3 ) The name comes from ``sawara ( suna-hara )'' in Japanese.  It 
means ``sandy, field.'' 

#4

In the beginning of the Meiji period, the former Tokugawa Shogunate 
forces landed on the coast of Washinoki.  It was the beggining of the 
Hakodate War.  The Hakodate War was the final phase of the Boshin War 
( civil war ).
 
The landing took place on December 4th, 1868.  A strong wind was 
blowing on the day.  The local strong winter wind is called Taba-
kaze. 

The commanding officers of the Tokugawa Shogunate were Takeaki Enomoto 
and Toshizou Hijikata.  They were aboard the battleships Kaiyou and 
Kaiten.  Kaiyou was made in the Netherlands.  Kaiten was made in 
Prussia ( now Germany ).  The battleship Kaiyou was sunk in Esashi.  
The battleship Kaiten was sunk in Hakodate.  

( For battleship Kaiyou, see Esashi Town in Hiyama sub-prefecture. ) 
( For the Boshin War, see Hakodate City in Oshima sub-prefecture. )
( For the local wind Taba-kaze, see Easahi Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )

#5

National Route 5 runs along a railway.  The highway connects Hakodate 
and Sapporo.  Its predecessor opened in 1873 ( Meiji 6 ).  The old 
route was called the Sapporo Main Road ( Sapporo-hondou ).  It crossed 
Uchiura Bay ( Volcano Bay ) by ferry.

There was a ferry line between Mori to Muroran. It operated from 1872 
to 1893 and again from 1908 to 1928.  It was called Shin-ran ( \u68EE
\u862D ) sea route ( by https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/
%E6%A3%AE%E6%B8%AF ). 
 
In 1878 ( Meiji 11 ), a British writer, Isabella Lucy Bird, stayed at 
an inn in Mori.  She was traveling from Hakodate to Biratori to see 
Ainu culture.  She documented Mori like this, ``Mori is a large village 
in the south of Volcano Bay, but the houses are about to collapse at 
any moment.''  But, she liked the view from the inn. 

( For the British writer, see Nanae Town in Oshima sub-prefecture,

#6

The Sawara branch route of the Hakodate Main Line connects Mori Station 
and Oonuma Station.  The branch route passes through the former Shikabe 
Town.  It was constructed during the Second World War to increase 
transportation capacity.

The branch route was constructed using two existing railroads ; Oshima-
kaigan-tetsudou ( Oshima Coastal Railway ) and Oonuma-dentetsu ( Oonuma 
Electric Railway ).  In June 1945, the branch route opened.  In August 
1945, Japan surrendered to the war.

#7

As for animal husbandry, there are swine farms in Mori Town.  They are 
managed by NH Foods ( Nippon Ham Foods ).  The company's processing 
plant is located in neighboring Yakumo Town.  As for food processing, 
Nichirei Foods produces croquettes and minced cutlets.  Maruha Nichiro 
produces salmon flakes and minced chicken. 

Nichirei Foods has a stone monument in the garden.  It commemorates the 
birth place of frozen food in Japan.  Their frozen food business 
started in 1920 ( Taishou 9 ). 

At the time, Kuzuhara-shoukai Co. ( now Nichirei Foods ) produced 
frozen fish using a charcoal gas engine.  The frozen fish was sold in 
Toukyou.  The company's freezing plant was constructed by American 
father-and-son engineers.  The company also owned reefer ships.

Regarding frozen food, the first home refrigerator was sold by GE 
( General Electric ) in the USA in the 1920s.  After the mid 1960s, 
Japanese companies produced a lot of refrigerators and exported them.

My parents used a Hitachi refrigerator.  It lasted 50 years.  The next 
one was broken in two years.  Nowadays, companies pursue profit.  
Refrigerators that never break are bad for them. 

#8

Hokkaidou Electric Power ( Hokuden ) runs a geothermal power plant in 
Mori Town.  It is located on the slope of Nigorikawa caldera.  
Geothermal power generation is suitable to the small volcanic country.  
There is no fuel cost.

But, geothermal power generation has these disadvantages ; ( 1 ) The 
output is small compared to the size of the building.  ( 2 ) 
Construction cost is high.  ( 3 ) Many volcanoes are located in 
national parks, so nature conservation laws restrict the construction. 

#9

Abe-shouten ( Abe Store ) started to sell Ika-meshi ( squid rice ) at 
Mori Station in 1941.  It was during the Second World War.  Trains 
stopped at Mori Station for a long tie because it was the junction of 
the Hakodate Main Line and the Oshima Coastal Railway ( Oshima-kaigan-
tetsudou ).

Squid rice became popular among young soldiers who stationed in 
Hokkaidou.  At the time, the catch of flying squid was good.  Also, 
squid rice can be cooked with a small amount of rice.  During the war, 
rice distribution was controlled. 
 
Today, many companies sell squid rice.  The original Abe-shouten's 
squid rice was not sold at souvenir shops, because the expiry date was 
one day.  So, it was sold through demonstration sales at department 
stores. 
 
The original Abe-shouten's squid rice became vacuum packed in 2016.  
Souvenir shops started selling the original squid rice at Sapporo 
Station and New Chitose Airport.  Incidentally, in Mori Town, squid-
shaped potteries of 4000-year-old were excavated in 2002.
 
As for department stores, there used to be department stores in front 
of large stations.  Many of them closed.  Large shopping malls with 
large parking areas opened in the former paddy fields.  Your 
grandmothers like to shop at department stores.  ( Department stores 
also manage out-of-store sales ( ``gaishou'' ) ).

LOCATION  

Yakumo ( ya-ku-mo ) ( \u516B\u96F2 ) is a town in Oshima sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  The area crosses the 
narrow part of the Oshima Peninsula.  It faces Uchiura Bay ( Funka-
wan ) and the Sea of Japan.  The name ``Funka-wan'' is a translation of 
``Volcano Bay'' into Japanese. 

The area was newly formed by the merger of Yakumo Town and Kumaishi 
Town in 2005.  The former Yakumo Town was located in the Pacific 
Ocean side.  The former Kumaishi Town was located in the Sea of Japan
side. 

( For Volcano Bay, see Oshima sub-prefecture. )

NAME 

The name ``Yakumo'' is Japanese.  It means ``overlapping clouds.''  
Yoshikatsu Tokugawa, who was the founder of Tokugawa Farm, named it.  
He quoted the name from an old poem.  ( Tokugawa Farm was the 
birthplace of Hokkaidou's famous souvenirs ; butter candy and carved 
wooden bear ( a bear eating a salmon ) ).  #1
 
The name ``Kumaishi'' comes from ``kuma-us-i'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``fish drying site, many, place.''  The Kanji characters for 
Kumaishi ( \u718A\u77F3 ) mean ``bear, stone.''

With the merger of two towns in 2005, Futami ( \u4E8C\u6D77 ) county 
wasestablished.  The name ``Futa-mi'' is Japanese, which means ``two 
seas.''  It signifies the Pacific Ocean and the Sea of Japan. 
 
HISTORY 

In the early Edo era, the Matsumae Domain built a guardhouse ( ``ban-
sho'' ) in Kumaishi on the Sea of Japan side.  It was the northern end 
of Japanese territory. 
 
On the Pacific Ocean side, there were trading places ( ``basho'' ).  
They were located in Yuurappu and Nodaoi.
 
In the mid Edo era, the Matsumae Domain built a guardhouse ( ``ban-
sho'' in Yamukushinai ( later called as Yamakoshinai ).  It became the 
northern end of Japanese territory on the Pacific Ocean side.

The trading place Yuurappu was renamed Yamakoshinai.  The main village 
was moved from Nodaoi to Otoshibe due to an increase in fishing 
immigrants.  ( On the Hakodate Main Line, there are stations of 
Otoshibe, Nodaoi, Yamakoshi ( the former Yamakoshinai Station ), and 
Yakumo from south to north. ) 

( Nodaoi = \u91CE\u7530\u8FFD.  Yuurappu = \u904A\u697D\u90E8.
  Yamakoshinai = \u5C71\u8D8A\u5185.  Yamakoshi = \u5C71\u8D8A. 
  Otoshibe = \u843D\u90E8. ) 

The following is the history of Yakumo Town after the Meiji period ;

OLD YAKUMO TOWN ; In 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), Yamakoshinai branch of the 
Hokkaidou Development Commission ( Kaitaku-shi ) was established.  In 
1878 ( Meiji 11 ), Tokugawa Farm opened.  In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), 
administrative offices ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) were established in 
Yamakoshinai and Otoshibe villages.
 
In 1890 ( Meiji 23 ), the administrative office of Yamakoshinai village 
was moved to Yakumo village.  In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Yakumo Village 
( 2nd-class municipality ) was established.  In 1903 ( Meiji 22 ), 
Yamakoshinai Station and Yakumo Station on the Hokkaidou Railway ( now 
Hakodate Main Line ) opened.  

In 1907 ( Meiji 40 ), Yakumo Village ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established.  In 1915 ( Taishou 4 ), Otoshibe Village ( 2nd-class 
municipality ) was established.  In 1919 ( Taishou 8 ), Yakumo Town was 
established.  In 1957 ( Shouwa 32 ), Yakumo Town merged Otoshibe 
Village.

( For 1st-class and 2nd-class municipalities, see the note of Matsumae 
  Town. )

OLD KUMAISHI TOWN ; In 1973 ( Meiji 6 ), administrative offices 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) were established in three villages ; Kumaishi, 
Ainumanai, and Tomarikawa.  These were a continuation of village chiefs 
from the Edo era.

In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), three villages were merged and Kumaishi 
Village ( 2nd-class municipalities ) was established.  In 1962 ( Shouwa 
37 ), Kumaishi Town was established.  Herring fishing flourished in 
Kumaishi from the Edo era to the Meiji period.  The peak of Kumaishi's 
population was 10,049 in 1962 ( Shouwa 37 ).  

( Ainumanai = \u76F8\u6CBC\u5185.  Tomarikawa = \u6CCA\u5DDD. )

NOTE
 
#1

Yoshikatsu Tokugawa asked land granting because he intended to make 
jobs for the former samurais in the Owari Domain ( now Aichi 
Prefecture ).  His offer was accepted in 1879 ( Meiji 11 ) and Tokugawa 
Farm was established in Yuurappu district in Yamakoshinai village.  The 
area was 496 hectares.

The name ``Yuurappu'' comes from ``yu-rap'' in Ainu language.  It 
means ``hot spring, down.''
 
In 1881 ( Meiji 14 ), Yuurappu district was separated from Yamakoshinai 
village and became a new village.  At the time, Yoshikatsu Tokugawa 
named it Yakumo.  The name reffered to the oldest poem in the country.  
The lyrics are as follows ;

``Yakumo-tatsu izumo-yaegaki tsumagomi-ni yaegaki-tsukuru 
  sono-yaegaki-wo.''
( Overlapping clouds stand, fences within fences in Izumo, to live 
  with my wife, make fences within fences, the fences within fences ).

It is said that the poem was read by Susanoo-no-mikoto, who was a god 
of mythology.  The lyrics are mysterious.  Actually, it is an old song 
for weddings.

Tokugawa Farm started Western-style agriculture including dairy 
farming.  In winter, farmers on the farm carved wooden bears.  The 
wooden bears became a famous souvenir from Hokkaidou.  The prototype of 
the wooden bears was a carved wooden bear that Yoshichika Tokugawa 
bought in Switzerland in 1922.

As of 1927 ( Shouwa 2 ), Tokugawa Farm owned 5,083 hectares of land 
including forests.  After World War II,  the Allied Powers ( US Army ) 
ordered land reform throughout the country.  Tokugawa Farm was 
demolished.

( For large farms in the Meiji period, see Niki Town in Shiribeshi sub-
  prefecture. )

As for the land reform after World War II, troubles continued.  For 
example, a family in a rural area owns 2-tan ( 20 ares ) of paddy 
fields, but they don't cultivate.  They lent the land.

( continue ) --> The family was informed just before the enactment of

farmer.  The landlord still says, ``I hate them ( the family ) very

#2

National Route 277 runs near the town hall of Yakumo.  It crosses 
Unseki Pass and reaches Kumaishi.  Unseki Pass divides Pacific Ocean 
side and the Sea of Japan side. 

The name ``Un-seki'' means ``cloud, stone'' in Japanese.  The 
word ``un ( cloud )'' means ``kumo'' in Ya-kumo and the word ``seki 
( stone )'' means ``ishi'' in Kuma-ishi.
 
The town hall of the former Kumaishi Town was located along the Sea of 
Japan.  National Route 229 ( Hiyama-kokudou ) passes along the

From the town hall of the former Kumaishi Town hall to the current

#3  

During the Taishou period ( 1912-1926 ), farmers in Yakumo cultivated 
mainly potatoes.  The potatoes were processed into potato starch and 
exported abroad.  The export declined due to the Great Depression.  
After the 1930s, dairy farming increased. 

Nowadays, Yakumo is the main dairy farming region in south Hokkaidou.  
The main breed of dairy cow is Holstein.  It is the largest breed among 
cows and cattle.  ( Cows are for milk and cattle are for beef. )
 
Holstein is a black and white cow.  Holstein was bread in Netherlands. 
It was named after a place in Germany.  In Japanese, it was called 
``roppaku-gyuu,'' which means ``six-white, cow,'' because there are six 
white spots around its nose. 

( For dairy cow, see Eniwa City in Ishikari sub-prefecture. )

#4

As for fishery in Yakumo, the main catches in Pacific Ocean side are 
farmed scallops, salmon, right-eye flounder, and farmed Konbu kelp. The 
main catches in the Sea of Japan side are walleye pollock, squid, and 
farmed abalone.  For the abalone culture, deep sea water is used.  Deep 
sea water is rich in minerals and low in bacteria. 
  
Abalone is expensive.  The shell is hard.  Inside the shell is mother-
of-peal ( ``shinju-sou'' ).  The shell has holes.  They are for 
breathing.  ( They are not eyes.  Scallops have eyes. )

#5

As for chicken, I think chicken tastes better in Hokkaidou.  Chickens 
can grow healthy under a cool climate, without sweating.

In Hokkaidou, fried chicken is called ``zangui.''  The word would have 
come from Chinese.  In Japanese, fried chicken is generally 
called ``kara-ague.''  It means ``Tang ( old China ), deep fried.''

#6

A meat processing factory is located in Yakumo Town.  Swine farms are 
located in mountainous areas in Mori Town and Imakane Towns.  In the 
past, Brazilians worked at the meat processing factory.  There was a 
Brazilian restaurant in the center of Yakumo Town. 

Masao Kanamori who instructed a brass band club at a local high school 
said ; ``I sometimes went to the Brazilian restaurant.  There were 
bossa nova concerts.  I was hooked on bossa nova.''

Students played musical instruments at Faculty of Comparative Culutre, 
University of Tsukuba.  Two of them also recommended ``Aguas de marco 
( aguasu-de-maruso ).''  It's a bossa nova album from Rio de Janeiro.  
The name means ``Waters of March.''  ( The name ``Rio de Janeiro'' 
means ``River, of, January.'' )

NAME 

The name ``Oshamanbe'' is derived from ``o-samam-pet'' in Ainu 
language, which means ``river mouth, bend, river.''  Or, it is derived 
from ``o-samampe'' in Ainu language, which means ``river mouth,

HISTORY 

During the Edo era, in 1669, the Ainu forces advanced from Hidaka to 
Matsumae.  It was the revolt of Shakushain ( Shakushain-no-tatakai ).  
The leader of the Ainu forces was Shakushain, a chief of Ainu people.  
The Ainu forces were defeated by the gun corps of the Matsumae Domain 
along the Kun-nui River.  #4 
 
In 1773, the Matsumae Domain built a guardhouse ( ``ban-sho'' ) and 
Inari Shrine.  ( Incidentally, in 2022, water gushed out from the Inari 
Shrine's grounds up to a height of 30 meters.  It was an old well for 
drilling natural gus. )

In 1864, a trading place ( ``basho'' ) in Yamakoshinai was abolished, 
and Yamakoshinai village and Oshamanbe village were established. 
Yamakoshinai was located in what is now Yakumo Town.
 
After the Meiji period, in 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), Oshamanbe branch of 
Hakkaidou Development Commission was established.  In 1873 ( Meiji 6 ), 
a village chief ( ``kochou'' ) was appointed.  It was a continuation of 
village chiefs from the Edo era.  In 1903 ( Meiji 36 ), Oshamanbe 
Station on the Hokkaidou Railway opened ( now Hakodate Main Line ).  

In 1906 ( Meiji 39 ), Oshamanbe Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established.  In 1923 ( Taishou 12 ), Oshamanbe Village ( 1st-class 
municipality ) was established.  The Nagawa Line opened ( now Muroran 
Main Line ).  Oshamanbe Station became a junction.  In 1943 ( Shouwa 
18 ), Oshamanbe Town was established.

Shakushain was a chief of Ainu people.  Before the revolt of

Then, Ainu chiefs united and revolted in many places.  The Ainu forces

Reading this history, I thought, ``( 1 ) People unite when they have a

not beat the guns.'' 

The background of the revolt was the trade imbalance between Ainu

low prices.  Also, the eruption of Mt. Tarumae in 1667 may have caused 
food shortages.

Kaminokuni ( ka-mi-no-ku-ni ) ( \u4E0A\u30CE\u56FD ) is a town in 
Hiyama sub-prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  The area faces

NAME 

The name ``Kami-no-kuni'' is Japanese.  It means ``upper, of, 
country.''  In the 15th century, the Andou clan in Tsugaru ( now Aomori 
Prefecture ) entered Ezo ( now Hokkaidou Prefecture ).  They called the 
western part ``Kami-no-kuni'' and the eastern part ``Shimo-no-kuni.''  
The name ``Shimo-no-kuni'' means ``country, of, lower.'' 

Kami-no-kuni was near to the Andou clan's homeland.  Shimo-no-kuni was 
far from it.  Shimo-no-kuni was located in what are now Hakodate and 
Hokuto cities.  In their homeland, the Andou clan also governed Kami-
no-kuni and Shimo-no-kuni.  ( Their homeland was what are now Aomori 
and Akita prefectures. ) 

HISTORY 

In the early 13th century, the Andou clan traded with Ainu people.  
They also managed exiles from the Kamakura Shogunate.  In the mid 15th 
century, the Nanbu clan advanced to Tsugaru, so Masasue Andou and his 
vassals escaped to Ezo.  #1 

Masasue Andou built 12 forts in Ezo.  In the area what is now 
Kaminokuni Town, there were two forts ; Hanazawa-date and Hiishi-date. 
Around the forts were Japanese settlements.  They were one of the 
oldest Japanese settlements in Ezo.  #2  #3
 
Koshamain's War occurred in 1457.  Koshamain was an Ainu chief.  
Nobuhiro Takeda, who was a vassal of the Andou clan, ended the war.  In 
the 16th century, the main fort in Ezo moved from Kaminokuni to 
Matsumae. 

During the Edo era ( 1603-1868 ), Kaminokuni was governed by the 
Matsumae Domain.  Kaminokuni was a village of Buddhist temples, Shintou 
shrines, and fishing. 

( For the Andou clan, see Hakodate City and Nanae Town in Oshima sub-
  prefecture. )  
( For the oldest Japanese village, see Fukushima Town in Oshima sub-
  prefecture. )

After the Meiji period, in 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), administrative offices 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) were established in Kaminokuni and Shiofuki 
villages.  Those were a continuation of village chiefs from the Edo 
era.

In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), three administrative offices for seven villages 
were established ; One was for the villages of ( 1 ) Kaminokuni, ( 2 ) 
Kita, and ( 3 ) Oodome ; One was for the villages of ( 4 ) Shiofuki and 
( 5 ) Kinoko ; One was for the villages of ( 6 ) Ishizaki and ( 7 ) 
Chiisago.

In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Kaminokuni Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of seven villages.  In 1967 ( Shouwa 42 ), 
Kaminokuni Town was established.

#1

As for exiles in the early 13th century, thieves, pirates, and

gazetteers of Ezo.  

More than five centuries before these explores, Japanese people lived

Hokkaidou is a large island.  The climate is too cold to live for 
Japanese people before the Edo era. 

#2

This is about Koshamain's War.  In 1456, an Ainu man asked a Japanese 
blacksmith to make a large knife.  The knife is called ``makiri'' in 
Japanese.

When the knife was made, the Ainu man quarreled over the quality and 
the price.  The blacksmith got angry and stabbed the Ainu man to death

In 1457, Ainu people united under Koshamain and attacked Japanese

#3

Masasue Andou built 12 forts in Ezo ( old Hokkaidou ) and returned to

Among the vassals of the Andou clan were the Kakizaki clan and the

Edible mountain plants grown in snowy regions are large and of good

The temple is located at the foot of the hill where where Fort Hanazawa

( For Hou-ryu-uji temple, see Hiyama sub-prefecture. )
 
Temples and shrines last longer than ordinary houses.  The main 
buildings of temples and shrines don't have water facilities such as 
kitchens and bathrooms. 
 
There are many Buddhist temples and Shintou shrines in old towns.  
Kaminokuni is one of them.  Kaminokuni was the original place of the 
Matsumae Domain, which ruled Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ) during the Edo era.
 
In the past, monks in temples and priests in shrines were like civil 
servants.  They recorded people's names and place names.  Ordinary 
people couldn't read and write. In addition, in the past, many people 
were born and many people died.  Monks and priests treat funeral 
services.

#9

There are old legends in Kaminokuni Town.  Ookura herring is one of 
them.  The story has been told since the 1590s, before the Edo era ;

``In a certain year, people couldn't catch herring until the end of 
May.  The fishing season was almost over.  At the time, the elderly 
monk Shuukai Ookura said to the villagers, `I will pray for herring 
to come.  If you catch, please share them with me.  I will repair my 
temple.'

Villagers made a promise.  The monk tied up a sacred rope, fasted, and 
prayed.  One week later, seagulls covered the sea, Shikabe 
( albatross ) gathered on the shore, whales spurted water and made big 
waves.  Schools of herring came. 

The catch was larger than usual.  Villagers were very happy.  However, 
a perverse man refused to give his thanks to the monk, saying, `It's 
just late this year, not your prayer.'  He got into a fight with the 
old monk and the monk died.

Villagers prayed to the spirit of the monk.  After the incident, when a 
small number of herring washed up on the shore in June, villagers 
called them `Ookura herring'.''

These are titles of other legends ; ``Upside-down water pine ( yew ),'' 
``Beheaded pine,'' and ``The path of god.''  People may have made 
legends during the long winter.

LOCATION  

Esashi ( e-sa-shi ) ( \u6C5F\u5DEE ) is a town in Hiyama sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  The area faces the Sea of 
Japan.  The shape of the town is like the letter ``B.''  The narrow 
part is the mouth of the Assabu River.  ( Assabu Town is located in the 
narrow part. )

As for the original vegetation, the mountains were covered with Hiba 
trees ( a kind of cypress ).  In the Edo era, Hiba trees were actively 
felled.  The logs were transported down the Assabu River.  The county's 
name ``Hi-yama'' is Japanese, which means ``cypress mountain.''  #1

NAME

The name ``Esashi'' comes from ``e-sa-us-i'' in Ainu language, which 
means ``head, seashore, exist, place.''  The name stands for a cape 
( promontory ).  It signified Kamome island in the Port of Esashi.  
( Kamome island is a tied island. )  The etymology is the same as 
Esashi Town in Souya sub-prefecture.  #2

HISTORY

In 1216, Ubagami-daijinguu Shrine was established.  It's one of the 
oldest Shintou shrines in Hokkaidou.  In the late 17th century, during 
the Edo era, the Matsumae Domain built a port to transport cypress.  
#3  #4

Herring fishing thrived in the Edo era.  When fishing season began, 
fishermen gathered from Honshuu island.  The Port of Esashi was 
bustling.  Esashi became one of the largest ports of the Matsumae 
Domain along with Fukuyama ( Matsumae ) and Hakodate.  #5
 
In 1868 ( Meiji 1 ), during the Boshin War, the former Tokugawa 
Shogunate forces occupied Esashi.  In 1869 ( Meiji 2 ), the Meiji 
government forces recaptured Esashi.  They advanced to Hakodate and 
defeated the former Tokugawa Shogunate forces.

In 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), Esashi branch of Hokkaidou Development Commission 
( Kaitaku-shi ) was established.  Administrative offices ( ``kochou-
yakuba'' ) were established for 26 towns in the built-up area of 
Esashi.  These were a continuation of town chiefs from the Edo era.

In 1880 ( Meiji 13 ), a county office ( ``gun-yakusho'' ) for Hiyama 
and Nishi counties was established.  In 1897 ( Meiji 30 ), Hiyama sub-
prefecture ( Hiyama-shichou ) was established.  The county office of 
Hiyama and Nihsi was abolished.

In 1900 ( Meiji 33 ), Esashi Town ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of 26 towns in Esashi and Gokatte village.  
In 1955 ( Shouwa 30 ), Esashi Town merged Tomari Village.  The shape of 
Tomari Village was like the letter ``B.''  #6  #7

In the past, herring shoals spawned off the coast of Hokkaidou from 
March to May.  Herring were processed into herring meal and transported 
to Oosaka by North-bound ship ( Kitamae-bune ).  Fertilizer wholesalers 
purchased herring meal.  North-bound ships departed from Oosaka and 
sailed along the Seto Inland Sea, the Sea of Japan, and Hokkaidou.

In the Edo era, herring meal and dried sardines were called ``kinpi,''  
which means ``gold fertilizer'' or ``money fertilizer'' in Japanese.  
Farmers started to buy fertilizer with money.  Previously, farmers had 
used leaf litter, livestock manure, and human manure as fertilizers.

``Kinpi,'' the so-called money fertilizer, was used for cultivating 
commercial crops, such as indigo, rapeseed, and cotton.  Those crops 
were mainly cultivated in western Japan.

Herring caught in Hokkaidou were also processed into ``migaki'' ( dried 
herring for eat ) and transported to ``naichi ( main island ).''

#11

As for old buildings in Esashi, the former Nakamura's residence has 
been designated as National Important Cultural Property ( Juuyou-bunka-
zai ).  The residence was built from the end of the Edo era to the 
Meiji period  ( more than 150 years ago ).  The owner was a merchant 
from Oumi ( now Shiga Prefecture ).

The Nakamura's residence was a shipping wholesaler.  Buildings in the 
residence are connected by passages.  The architecture is called 
Shipping Wholesaler Architecture ( Kaisen-donya-kenchiku ). 

In the past, along the Port of Esashi, shipping wholesalers were lined 
up, including the Nakamura's residence.  The wholesalers spanned piers 
on the water and transported cargo to ships.

The ships owned by Oumi merchants were called Kitamae-bune ( North-
bound ships ).  The hometown of Oumi merchants was what is now Higashi-
oumi City in Shiga Prefecture.  Esashi Town and Higashi-oumi City are 
sister cities. 
 
``North-bound ship'' was also called ``Sengoku-bune.''  The word ``sen-
goku-bune'' means ``1,000-koku, ship'' in Japanese.  ``Koku'' is an 
old unit of volume. One ``koku'' equals 150 kg in case of rice.  So, 
the ship carried 150 tons of rice.  ( Larger ships than this size were 
prohibited by the Tokugawa Shognate. )

One ``koku'' of rice was worth about one ``ryou'' of money in the mid 
Edo era.  So, the sales of one voyage per North-bound ship ( 1,000-koku 
ship ) amounted to 1,000-ryou of money.  One ``ryou'' of money was 
equivalent to about 50,000 yen today.

In the past, tree felling started in early winter.  The forest floor

move smoothly in winter.  ( In summer, bamboo grass and grass are 
plentiful. )

Woodcutters used sleighs to haul logs to the river.  In spring, melting 
snow flooded the river.  Woodcutters floated the logs down the river.

The woodcutter in Assabu brought Shishi-odori dance ( Dear dance ) from 
their homeland.  The dance became an annual event at Hiyama guardhouse.
( Guardhouses in Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ) were called ``ban-sho'' in 
Japanese.  Those were run by the Matsumae Domain. )
	
A large forest fire occurred in 1695.  Subsequently, the Matsumae 
Domain banned logging from 1770 to 1807 to prevent deforestation.  
Again, a large forest fire occurred in 1876.

Very few natural forests remain.  Today, tourists see huge forests in 
Hokkaidou and think they are natural, however, those were felled and 
burned in the past.

In Assabu Town, a natural forest has designated as National Natural 
Monument ( Tennen-kinen-butsu ) since 1928.  It's ``Uzura-gawa-goyou-
matsu-jisei-hokugen-chitai ( Northern Limit of White Pine Natural 
Growing Area along the Uzura River ).''

( For another natural forest, see Esashi Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )

As for snowy mountains, a female member of Hokkaidou University 
Mountaineering Club said, ``When it snows, we can go into mountains 
that we cannot reach in summer.  In summer, bamboo grass ( `sasa' ) 
and dwarf pine trees ( `haimatsu' ) block the way.''  Mosquitoes and 
bears also appear in summer. 
 
As for sleigh and sled, in Sapporo City, mothers move their children 
using plastic sleds.  People have used sleigh and sled since antiquity
( anti'-quity ). 

( For sleigh, see Naie Town in Sorach sub-prefecture. )

defeating the former Tokugawa Shogunate, and bringing the war to an

Otobe Town belongs to Nishi county.  The Kanji characters for Nishi

The name was decided in the beginning of the Meiji period.

Takeshirou Matsuura, an explorer of Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ), suggested

was located in the west of the Oshima Peninsula.  His suggestion was an

#2

The name ``Otohe'' means ``river mouth, swamp, exist, thing'' in Ainu 
language.  As the name suggests, there were many swamps and ponds in 
the lower reaches of the Otohe River ( now Hime River ).  They were 
reclaimed and turned into paddy fields and a built-up area.

The current river name ``Hime'' means ``princess'' or ``noble lady'' in 
Japanese.  It refers to Shizuka-gozen in the case of Otobe Town.  
Shizuka-gozen was a ``mekake ( concubine )'' of Yoshitsune Minamoto.

Yoshitsune Minamoto was a commander of the Minamoto clan in the 12th 
century.  He was killed in battle in Oushuu ( now Touhoku region ), but 
many legends mention that he fled to Ezo ( now Hokkaidou ).  This is 
one ofe Yoshitsune legends in Otobe ;

``Shizuka-gozen came Otohe in search of Yoshitsune.  She could not find 
him, because he had escaped far inland.  She was disappointed and 
committed suicide by drowning in the river.''  So, the river was named 
the Hime River after Shizuka-gozen.

I guess that the legend was about the death of a courtesan ( ko'u-ti-
zan ) in the end of Edo era or in the Meiji Period.  ( Cf. There is 
Oiran-buchi in Minami Ward, Sapporo City, Ishikari sub-prefecture.  The

In the past, Okushiri was the island of alcohol prohibition.  ( Alcohol 
prohibition is called ``kinshu'' in Japanese. )  When the herring catch 
was poor, fishermen drunk, and quarreled too much.  So, village chiefs 
( ``kochou'' ) banned drinking alcohol.  Prohibition lasted from 1885 
to 1890.

Today, rice wine ( ``sake'' ) and wine are the specialties of Okushiri. 
There even is an ``Ordinance to promote toasts with Okushiri's local

#2

In July 1993, Okushiri earthquake happened and killed 230 people.  The 
magnitude was 7.8.  The disaster was due to tidal waves 
( ``tsunami'' ), fires, and landslides.  Geologically, Okushiri island 
is located near the boundary between the Eurasian Plate and North 
American Plate.  
 
Being close to the epicenter, the first tidal wave arrived two to three 
minutes after the earthquake.  Tidal waves killed 198 people.  Soon 
after the earthquake, television channels broadcast live footage from 
the sky.  I watched on TV that Aonae district was burning at that 
night.  

In 1998, the town declared ``completion of recovery.''  In 2001, the 
Tsunami Museum opened in Aonae district.  
 
* * *

As for tidal waves, the Sanriku Coast in the Touhoku region was hit by 
tidal waves.  During the massve tidal waves in 2011, all children at an 
elementary school along the coast could escape.  Grandparents taught 
them the phrase ``tsunami-tendenko ( tidal wave, alone ).''  If a large 
earthquake occurs, you must escape alone as soon as possible. 

Earthquakes, tidal waves, eruptions, and torrential downpours are 
natural phenomena.  These are a cycle that has continued for millions 
of years.  The cycle shaped the land and eroded.  Without human 
presence, disasters don't occur.  Disasters occur because humans 
perceive them as such. 

I am not part of nature.  I am the subject that perceives nature.  I 
see the rain and think, ``Pluit, ergo sum ( It's raining, therefore I 
am ).''

The name ``Kita-hiyama'' is Japanese.  It means ``north, Hiyama.''  
The name ``Hiyama'' means ``cypress mountain.''  

The name ``Setana'' comes from ``seta-rupespe-nay'' in Ainu language.  
It means ``dog, cross, river.''  It signified a place near the mouth of 
the Baba River.  Large rocks stand near the mouth of the river.  ( The 
Baba River flows through the center of the old Setana Town. ) 

The rocks are Sanbon-sugui-iwa Rock and  Rousoku-iwa Rock.  ( In 
Japanese, ``sanbon-sugi'' means ``three ceders'' and ``rousoku'' means 
``candle.'' )  Sanbon-sugui-iwa Rocks stand from the sea and Rousoku-
iwa Rock stands at the Port of Setana.  They look like dogs.  #2

( For the name ``Hiyama,'' see Hiyama sub-prefecture. )
( Taisei = \u5927\u6210.  Kudou = \u4E45\u9060.  Kaitorima = 
  \u8C9D\u53D6\u6F97.  Kita-hiyama = \u5317\u6A9C\u5C71.  Setana = 
  \u702C\u68DA. )

HISTORY 

In the Edo era, the Matsumae Domain established three trading places 
( ``basho'' ) for fishing.  They were located in Kudou, Futoro, and 
Setanai ( from south to north ).  The places thrived from herring 
fishing.  ( Futoro was located at the mouth of the Futoro River.  It 
flows through the former Kita-hiyama Town. ) 

After the Meiji period, three counties were established in 1869 
( Meiji 2 ).  They were Kudou, Futoro, and Setana counties.  The 
following is the history of the former three towns ;

OLD TAISEI TOWN ; In 1872 ( Meiji 5 ), Kudou branch of the Hokkaidou 
Development Commission was established.  In 1880 ( Meiji 13 ), the 
administrative office ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) of Issouma village was 
established.  It covered other five villages ; Oota, Uekotan, Sansouma, 
Hikata-tomari, and Yunoshiri.

In the same year, the administrative office of Hiratanai was 
established.  It covered other two villages ; Kaitorima and Nagaiso. 
In 1881 ( Meiji 14 ), the administrative office of Kudou village was 
established by the merger of Issouma, Sansouma, and Hikata-tomari.

In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Kudou Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Kudou, Oota, Uekotan, and Yunoshiri.  In 
1923 ( Taishou 12 ), Kaitorima Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Kaitorima, Hiratanai, and Nagaiso.

In 1955 ( Shouwa 30 ), Taisei Village was established by the merger of 
Kudou and Kaitorima.  In 1966 ( Shouwa 41 ), Taisei Town was 
established.

( Issouma = \u4E00\u8258\u6F97.  Oota = \u592A\u7530.  Uekotan = 
  \u4E0A\u53E4\u4E39.  Sansouma = \u4E09\u8258\u6F97.  Hikata-tomari = 
  \u65E5\u65B9\u6CCA.  Yunoshiri = \u6E6F\u5C3B.  Hiratanai = 
  \u5E73\u7530\u5185.  Nagaiso = \u9577\u78EF. )

OLD KITA-HIYAMA TOWN ; In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), an administrative office 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) was established for four villages in Futoro 
county ; Futoro, Gorota, Raruishi, and Udomari.  In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), 
the administrative office of Higashi-setana village was established.  
It was separated from Setana village.

In 1906 ( Meiji 39 ), Futoro Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of Futoro, Gorota, Raruishi, and Udomari.  In 
the same year, Higashi-setana Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established. 

In 1953 ( Shouwa 28 ), Higashi-setana Town was established.  In 1955 
( Shouwa 30 ), Kita-hiyama Town was established by the merger of 
Higashi-setana Town and Futoro Village.

( Futoro = \u592A\u6AD3.  Gorota = \u53E4\u6AD3\u591A.  Raruishi = 
  \u826F\u7460\u77F3.  Udomari = \u9D5C\u6CCA.  Higashi-setana = 
  \u6771\u702C\u68DA. )

OLD SETANA TOWN ; In 1879 ( Meiji 12 ), two administrative offices 
( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) were established for five villages in Setana 
county ; Setana, Baikatsu, Nakauta, Abura, and Shimauta.  In 1897 
( Meiji 30 ), Toshibetsu village was separated from Setana village.  It 
was what is now Imakane Town, Hiyama sub-prefecture.

In 1902 ( Meiji 35 ), Setana Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was 
established by the merger of five villages in Setana county.  Higashi-
setana village was separated from Setana Village.  ( This means that an 
administrative office was built in Higashi-setana village.  Higashi-
setana was merged to Kita-hiyama. ) 

In 1919 ( Taishou 8 ), Setana Village ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established.  In 1921 ( Taishou 10 ), Setana Town was established.

( Baikatsu = \u6885\u82B1\u90FD.  Nakauta = \u4E2D\u6B4C.  Abura = 
  \u867B\u7F85.  Shimauta = \u5CF6\u6B4C. )

TOWN HALL, TRAFFIC

The town hall of Setana is located near the mouth of the Toshibetsu 
River ( Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River ).  It used to be the town hall of 
Kita-hiyama Town.  National Route 229 ( Hiyama-kokudou ) runs along the 
Sea of Japan.  #3

National Route 230 ( Pirika-kokudou ) separates from National Route 229 
near the town hall.  It crosses the Oshima Peninsula along the 
Toshibetsu River and the Kunnui River.

Formerly, the Setana Line of the Japanese National Railways 
( Kokutetsu ) ran along National Route 230.  The railway connected 
Setana Station and Kun-nui Station in Oshamanbe Town.

The Port of Setana has a ferry line to Okushiri island.  Windmills 
near the port are the first offshore windmills in the country.  In the 
built-up area of the former Setana Town, there was a clinic run by 
Guinko Oguino.  She was the first female medical doctor in the 
country.  #4

NOTE  

#1

Among Hokkaidou's northern tip, southern tip, eastern tip, and western 
tip, the western tip is the most difficult to visit.  It's a steep sea 
cliff at Cape Obana.  Including small islands, the western tip is 
Oshima-ooshima island in Matsumae Town, Oshima sub-prefecture.  It's an 
uninhabited island. 

Oota Shrine at Cape Obana is one of the oldest Shintou shrines in 
Hokkaidou.  It was established around 1441.  It is one of the most 
difficult shrines to visit. 

The shrine is located at the top of a rocky mountain.  Bears live. 
Visitors are like climbers.  They will pray at the shrine for a safety 
descent down the mountain.  In the past, mountaineering was a religious 
training. 

#2

On the map published by Takeshirou Matsuura in 1859, Haha-gawa ( now 
Baba River ) and Setaruheshi-nai are written together ( http://
koukita.github.io/touzaiezo/map/ ).

Tadashi Okudaira wrote that the upper course of the Baba River was 
called the Setanai River.  Sanbon-sugui-iwa Rocks, which Ainu people 
saw as dogs ( ``seta'' ), are located at the mouth of the Baba River 
( T. Okudaira et al. 2007 ).

I asked the town hall where the Setani River was.  They answered that 
it might be the current Toshibetsu River ( Shiribeshi-toshibetsu 
River ).  They showed a map of Setanai Basho ( Setanai trading place ) 
as evidence.  The map was drawn in the Ansei period ( 1855-1860 ) of 
the Edo era. 

Hiyama and Oshima sub-prefectures have a long history of Japanese 
settlement.  Some river names and place names were changed from Ainu 
language to Japanese.  These are examples ;
( 1 ) The Baba River in Setana Town, which may have been called the 
Setanai River.
( 2 ) The Hime River in Otobe Town, whose old name was the Otohe River. 
( 3 ) The Torizaki River in Mori Town, whose old name was the Oniushi 
River.

( For typical river names in Ainu language, see Oshamanbe Town in 
  Oshima sub-prefecture. )

As for the name ``Seta-nai'' ( ``dog, river'' in Ainu language ), there 
is an Ainu legend ; ``Once upon a time, large dogs came down from 
the mountains.  They were messengers of god.  They went into the sea 
and turned into rocks.''

During a bus excursion, we stopped in front of Sanbon-sugui-iwa Rocks 
and ate lunch boxes.  One of the students met an elderly man walking 
along a national highway.

 He said,  ``During Okushiri earthquake, tidal waves ( ``tsunami'' )

( For Okushiri earthquake, see Okushiri Town in Hiyama sub-

#3

National Route 229 runs along the coast.  But, near Cape Obana, which

#4  

Guinko Oguino ( 1851-1913 ) was born in Tawarase village ( now Kumagaya 
City, Saitama Prefecture ).  In 1885, she passed medical practitioner's 
examination and opened a clinic for gynecology and obstetrics.  She 
became the first female medical doctor in the country.  Her clinic was 
located in Yushima, Toukyou.

In 1891, her husband, Yukiyoshi Shikata, started developing Immanuel 
district.  Immanuel was located in what is now Imakane Town, Hiyama 
sub-prefecture.  It was in the upper reaches of the Shiribeshi-
toshibetsu River.  The area was developed by Christians.  

( For the name ``Immanuel,'' see Imakane Town in Hiyama sub-
  prefecture. )

In 1894, Guinko Oguino moved to Immanuel.  Then, her husband failed 
in development and became a missionary.  In 1897, she moved to Setana 
and opened a clinic.  ( Setana is located near the mouth of the 
Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River. )

In 1903, she went to Sapporo.  She wanted to open a larger clinic in 
Sapporo, however, she was told that her medical technique was 20 years' 
behind.  She was disappointed and returned to Setana.  In 1905, her 
husband died. In 1908, she returned to Toukyou. 

As for a medical doctor, I lived in a teachers' apartment in Sapporo.  
Next to the apartment was a clinic for internal medicine and 
pediatrics.  The elderly doctor said, ``I was originally internist.  
When I added pediatrics in this clinic, my friends scorned me.  But, I 
had to raise my children.''

He was proud that his three sons became medical doctors.  When I went 
to the clinic with the influenza, he said, ``The latest machine to 
test the influenza is expensive.  The debt will be left to my son, ha, 
ha, ha ( laugh ).''

After his sudden death, one of his sons came from a hospital and 
succeeded the clinic for about a year.  Then, the clinic was demolished 
and an apartment was built.

LOCATION   

Imakane ( i-ma-ka-ne ) ( \u4ECA\u91D1 ) is a town in Hiyama sub-
prefecture, south Hokkaidou ( ``dou-nan'' ).  Imakane is located 
inland in the Oshima Peninsula.

The area corresponds to the middle to upper reaches of the Shiribeshi-
toshibetsu River, which flows to the Sea of Japan.  ( The downstream 
area is Setana Town. )  ( The Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River is called 
the Toshibetsu River by local residents. )

NAME 

The name ``Imakane'' is Japanese.  It is derived from two pioneers, 
Toujirou Imamura and Ishirou Kanamori ( Ima- and Kane- ).  Both 
were graduates of Toukyou Professional School ( now Waseda 
University ).  #1

HISTORY 

In the Edo era, the Matsumae Domain gathered gold dust in the upper 
reaches of the Shiribetsu-toshibetsu River.  In the Meiji period, there 
were agate and manganese mines.

In 1893 ( Meiji 26 ), two pioneers settled in what is now Imakane 
district.  In the same year, a group of Christians ( Kirisuto-kyou-
dantai ) settled in Immanuel district.  #2

In 1897 ( Meiji 30 ), the administrative office ( ``kochou-yakuba'' ) 
of Toshibetsu ( \u5229\u5225 ) village was established.  It was 
separated from Setana village.  In 1906 ( Meiji 39 ), Toshibetsu 
Village ( 2nd-class municipality ) was established.  

In 1929 ( Shouwa 4 ), Toshibetsu Village ( 1st-class municipality ) was 
established.  In 1947 ( Shouwa 22 ), Imakane Town was established.  At 
the time, Toshibetsu was renamed Imakane.

( For 1st-class and 2nd-class municipalities, see the note of Matsumae 
  Town. )
( For gold, see Naka-tonbetsu Town in Souya sub-prefecture. )
 
TOWN HALL, TRAFFIC

The town hall of Imakane is located along the middle reaches of the 
Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River.  National Route 230 ( Pirika-kokudou or 
Hana-ishi-douro ) runs.  #3  #4

In the past, the Setana Line ran along the Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River. 
The railway crossed the Oshima peninsula.  It connected Setana Station 
on the Sea of Japan side with Kun-nui Station on the Pacific Ocean 
side.  ( Setana Station was located in the former Setana Town.  Kun-nui 
Station on the Hakodate Main Line is located in Oshamanbe Town. ) 

INDUSTRY 

Distinctive industries in Imakane are agriculture, animal husbandry, 
and forestry.  STATISTICS OF AGRICULTURE = pasture 1,446 hectares in 
2015, paddy rice 1,414, beans ( soybeans 376, adzuki beans 208 ), 
potatoes 396, wheat 273, fodder 229, sugar beets 117, buckwheat 69,

( continue ) --> carrots 32, broccoli 24, barley 19, tomatoes 13, 
onions 5, and other grains 24 ; dairy cows 2,021 head in 2015 and hogs 
and pigs X ( concealed ).

Potatoes are sold with the trade-name ``Imakane-danshaku.''  As for 
animal husbandry, swine farms run by Nippon Ham Group are distributed 
in the mountains.  #5 

As for sightseeing, the Pirika Dam is located near Pirika Pass.  Its 
long long concrete dyke stands out along National Route 230.  The 
country sign ( gateway sign ) is Piiri and Piika, which are nicknames 
for sea cow fossils excavated from the dam construction site.  #6

NOTE   
 
#1

As for the name Imakane, local post office is called ``Imagane'' ( not 
Imakane ).  The former railroad station was also Imagane.  But, the 
name on the topographical map is Imakane.  ( Topographical maps are 
published by Geographical Survey Institute. )

Imakane's former name was Toshibetsu.  The name comes from ``tus-pet'' 
in Ainu language.  It means ``snake ( or rope ), river.''  Or, it comes 
from ``to-us-pet'' in Ainu language.  It means ``pond, many, river.''  
Both signified the Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River. 

The Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River was ranked as the cleanest river.  The 
ranking is listed in Current Water Quality of Class-A Rivers in the 
Country ( Zenkoku-ikkyuu-kasen- no-suishitsu-genkyou ) published by the 
Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism.

( For the cleanest rivers, see Rankoshi Town in Shiribeshi sub-
  prefecture. )

#2

A Christian group settled in Immanuel.  They wanted to make their 
utopia.  The name ``Immanuel'' means ``God with us'' in Hebrew.  
Immanuel was located in what is now Kamioka district.  The name ``Kami-
oka'' means ``god, hill'' in Japanese.

Guinko Oguino, the first female doctor in the country, came to 
Immanuel.  Her husband was a member of the Christian group.  An old map 
published in the Edo era shows the place name ``Imanpetsu.''  The 
Christian group may have imagined Immanuel.

( For Guinko Oguino, see Setana Town in Hiyama sub-prefecture. )

Hana-ishi district is located near the Pirika Dam.  The word ``hana-
ishi'' is Japanese.  It means ``agate ( a'get )'' in English and
``menou'' in Japanese.  Hana-ishi was the largest agate mine in the 
country in the Meiji period.  The agate was shipped to Fukui Prefecture 
and carved into ornaments ( Wakasa-menou-zaiku ). 

Nowadays, agate is mainly imported from Brazil.  When I visited Sao

shop.  He recommended me to buy agate chopstick rests.  He said, 
``Agate is artificially colored, but this is natural.''  It was brown.

#5

Danshaku is a potato cultivar for eat.  The word ``danshaku'' means 
``baron'' in Japanese.  ( Danshaku and May-queen are popular potato 
cultivars for eat.  Kona-fubuki is popular potato cultivar for 
processing.  The name ``Kona-fubuki'' means ``powder snowstorm.'' ) 

( For Danshaku potato, see Kucchan Town in Shiribeshi sub-prefecture. ) 
( For May-queen potato, see Assabu Town in Hiyama sub-prefecture. ) 
( For potato, see Shihoro Town in Tokachi sub-prefecture. )

Imakane potatoes have a reputation for quality.  The potato acreage per 
farm is smaller than that of the municipalities in Tokachi sub-
prefecture.  ( Tokachi is a major potato-producing region. )

A former high school teacher said ; ``Potatoes are cultivated in crop 
rotation in Tokachi.  Potatoes can be cultivated after potatoes in 
south Hokkaidou ( `dou-nan,' including Imakane Town ).''

In Tokachi, potatoes are cultivated after sugar beets.  Then, beans or 
wheat are cultivated after potatoes.  In Imakane, farmers take time on 
their smaller land.  They remove the so-called stray potatoes.

The word ``stray potato'' is translation of ``nora-imo'' in Japanese. 
Stray potatoes are the rest of the former harvest.  They give a bad 
impact on the next cultivation.  In Tokachi, stray potatoes are killed 
during the cold winter.  But, recently, not all stray potatoes are 
killed because of climate change.

#6

The Pirika Dam is 1,480 meters wide.  It's the widest concrete dam in 
the country.  The dam was constructed between 1975 and 1991 at the 
confluence of the three rivers ; the Shiribeshi-toshibetsu River, 
the Pirikabetsu River, and the Chuushibetsu River.

The Pirika Dam is used for power generation, irrigation, and flood 
control.  Driftwood collected from the dam lake is distributed free of 
charge for personal use, such as for wood-burning stoves and gardening.
 
Kur ( ku'-a ) Plaza Pirika is located in front of the dam.  It includes 
a hot spring, a ski resort, and a restaurant.  The specialty of the 
restaurant is dam curry.  Rice is blocking the curry like a dam.

( For curry and rice, see Hakodate City in Oshima sub-prefecture. )

In the upper reaches of the Pirikabetsu River, Okupirika hot spring is 
located along Prefectural Route 999.  The name ``Pirika-betsu'' means 
``beautiful river'' in Ainu language.  The Kanji characters for Pirika 
( \u7F8E\u5229\u6CB3 ) also means ``beautiful, benefit, river.''
